Monica Kelly, MS, RD, CDN, CNSC, for Montefiore Einstein

EN ESPAÑOL: Risotto de avena y calabaza rico en antioxidantes que refuerza el sistema inmunológico

Fall is a season of comfort and gratitude, and the food on the table often tells that story best. This Creamy Toasted Pumpkin Risotto with Parmesan and Fresh Sage is a wholesome dish that blends seasonal flavors with nourishing ingredients. Instead of traditional rice, this recipe uses fiber-rich oats, making it a unique and health-forward addition to any holiday or family menu.

The Power of Oats

Replacing rice with old-fashioned oats adds both flavor and nutrition. Oats are rich in soluble fiber, which supports digestion, helps lower cholesterol, and promotes steady blood sugar levels. Because oats are absorbed slowly into the bloodstream, they keep you feeling satisfied longer, supporting heart health, weight management, and lasting energy. For the best results, use old-fashioned oats rather than quick or steel-cut varieties to maintain the perfect creamy texture.

The Benefits of Pumpkin

Naturally sweet and smooth, pumpkin is high in fiber and packed with vitamins A, C, and K. These nutrients strengthen the immune system, support eye health, and promote healing. As a non-starchy vegetable, it is lower in calories while still offering a rich, satisfying texture that complements the oats beautifully.

Olive Oil and Onions: Flavor with Purpose

Cooking with olive oil provides heart-healthy unsaturated fats and anti-inflammatory benefits. It adds depth and richness without excess saturated fat. Onions enhance the dish with natural sweetness and antioxidants, offering gentle anti-inflammatory properties when enjoyed in moderation.

Warm, creamy, and full of seasonal flavor, this pumpkin oat risotto brings comfort, nourishment, and balance to every table.

Creamy Toasted Pumpkin Oat Risotto with Fresh Sage

Ingredients:

3 cups of fresh pumpkin or butternut squash, peeled and cut into 1-inch cubes

4 tablespoons olive oil, divided

1 large yellow onion coarsely chopped into small cubes

½ cup fresh sage leaves (half chopped, half left whole)

Pinch of kosher salt, to taste

Pinch of ground black pepper, to taste

1 cup old-fashioned oats (do not use quick oats or steel-cut oats)

4 cups low-sodium vegetable broth

4 tablespoons grated Parmesan cheese (optional)

Preparation:

Preheat the oven to 400°F. Line a large baking sheet with parchment paper.
Roast the pumpkin or squash, place the cubed pumpkin or butternut squash in a large mixing bowl. Drizzle with 2 tablespoons of olive oil, add the chopped sage, and season with salt and pepper. Toss well to coat evenly.
Spread the pumpkin or squash on the prepared baking sheet in a single layer. Roast for 25 to 30 minutes, or until tender and lightly browned on the edges.
Prepare the risotto base in a large Dutch oven or heavy-bottomed stockpot, heat the remaining 1 tablespoon of olive oil over medium heat. Add the onion and sauté for 3 to 4 minutes, until softened and translucent.
Add the old-fashioned oats and stir for 2 to 3 minutes to lightly toast them. Gradually pour in the vegetable broth, stirring occasionally. Reduce the heat to low and cook for about 20 minutes, or until the mixture thickens to a creamy consistency.
Roast the remaining whole sage leaves using one tablespoon of olive oil in a small sauté pan until crispy.
Prepare the pumpkin purée when the roasted pumpkin or squash is done, mash half of it in a bowl until smooth, keep the remaining half in whole pieces. Stir the purée into the oat mixture until well combined.
Finish and serve fold in the roasted pumpkin pieces. Top each serving with the crispy whole sage leaves, a pinch of black pepper before serving, and the grated parmesan cheese (if using).

Serves 5
