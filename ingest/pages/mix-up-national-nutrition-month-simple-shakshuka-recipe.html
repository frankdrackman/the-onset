Mix it up for National Nutrition Month with this simple shakshuka recipe

Jessica Shapiro, MS, RD, CDN, CDCES, for Montefiore
March 31, 2021, 3:34 p.m. ET

March is National Nutrition Month (#NationalNutritionMonth), which was created by The Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics. According to eatright.org, during the month of March “everyone is invited to learn about making informed food choices and developing healthful eating and physical activity habits.” This year, the theme is “Personalize Your Plate” and it emphasizes the fact that there's no “one size fits all” approach to your health and well-being.  We want to consider the uniqueness between each of us, including the differences of our “bodies, goals, backgrounds and tastes.” Furthermore, it is important to acknowledge that the approach to nutrition changes at each stage of life. One important goal is to find nutritious dishes that are right for you without sacrificing taste and enjoyment. Sometimes, that means exploring new cuisines that might be out of your comfort zone but could end up being some of your favorite meals. 

For this month’s recipe, I chose shakshuka, which literally translates to “mixture.” It’s a traditional dish from North Africa and the Middle East, and I know it from the Israeli tradition. It’s easy to make, very customizable, contains a lot of healthy ingredients and can be served for brunch, lunch and dinner, year-round. 

The eggs are the star of the dish, as they contain a ton of beneficial properties like protein and omega-3 fatty acids. One underrecognized and underappreciated nutrient in eggs is choline, which is necessary for brain health and memory. Choline is essential for everyone but pregnant women, kids and the elderly alike can benefit from more of it. Do not worry too much about the cholesterol in the yolk, as studies show that it does not cause the body’s cholesterol to increase in most people; it is the saturated and trans fats that come from animal products that are more of a concern. And in shakshuka, you can prepare the eggs any way you’d like — from runny to hard cooked.  

The tomatoes in the dish are an excellent source of lycopene, an antioxidant that supports heart and prostate health, yet only becomes bioavailable once tomatoes are cooked.  You can also add whichever fresh herbs you like, as they’re nutrient powerhouses that bring a large amount of antioxidant power. My favorite fresh herb to with this dish is parsley or basil.

In keeping with the theme of personalization, you can adjust this shakshuka recipe however you want. Some people like to add ½ lb. of lean ground meat for a heartier version. To do that, cook the meat first then stir it into the pot and let it simmer in the tomatoes for 15 minutes before adding the eggs. I like adding feta and fresh spinach for a Mediterranean flavor, and it’s delicious when served with pita or whole-grain bread for dipping. But most importantly, the taste is up to you — do whatever makes you happy and healthy! 

Shakshuka

Adapted from The Mediterranean Dish

Serves: 6 

Prep time: 10 minutes

Cook time: 30 minutes

Total time: 40 minutes

Ingredients

  • 2 tablespoon extra-virgin olive oil
  • 1 large yellow onion, chopped
  • 2 green peppers, chopped
  • 2 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 teaspoon ground coriander
  • 1 teaspoon sweet paprika
  • 1/2 teaspoon ground cumin
  • Red pepper flakes (optional)
  • Salt and pepper to taste
  • 6 cups chopped tomatoes (ripe tomatoes in the summer work great!)
  • 1/2 cup tomato sauce (canned or jarred) 
  • 6 large eggs
  • 1/2 cup chopped fresh herbs (parsley, cilantro, basil and/or mint leaves work well alone or in combination)

Directions

  1. Heat the olive oil in a large pan. Add veggies (not tomatoes) and spices. Cook for about 5 minutes, or until the veggies have softened.
  2. Add the tomatoes and tomato sauce. Bring them to boil then cover and simmer for about 15 minutes. Uncover and cook a little longer to reduce and thicken. Taste and adjust the seasoning as desired.
  3. Using a spoon, make 6 indentations, or "wells," spaced out in the tomato mixture. Gently crack an egg into each indention, making sure it doesn’t spread out all over the rest of the dish.
  4. Reduce the heat, cover the skillet and cook on low until the egg whites are set, or until desired doneness achieved. 
  5. Turn off the heat, uncover and add the fresh herbs. 
  6. Serve with pita or whole-grain bread, if desired.
