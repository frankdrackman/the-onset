Beat the pollen — combatting seasonal allergies

By By Denisa Ferastraoaru, MD, Co-Director, Montefiore Einstein Sinus and Allergy Center · 2 min read · Gánele al polen: plante cara a las alergias estacionales

Allergies hitting you hard? Try these proven tips and treatments.

Beat the pollen! 9 tips for combatting allergies this season

Pollen — from trees in spring, grasses in summer, and weeds in late summer and fall — is one of the most common causes of seasonal allergies. And because powdery pollen can travel up to 30 miles on a breeze, you can feel its effects whether you live outside or in a city. These 9 habits are proven to cut down on pollen in your home and on your person and can greatly reduce the severity of allergy symptoms.

Many people find they can manage their symptoms with antihistamines in tablet, capsule, liquid, or nasal-spray formulations available over the counter. Nasal sprays —beyond treating all that sniffling and sneezing, here’s a bonus: sprays can also help relieve itchy, watery, swollen, or red eyes (just remember that some sprays aren’t appropriate for long-term use, so ask your doctor). When sprays aren’t enough to relieve eye allergies, eye drops are your best bet, and in some cases your doctor might prescribe stronger anti-inflammatory eye drops. If your allergies are severe or really annoying, start taking your allergy medicine before the height of each pollen season begins, and take it every day if that’s what your doctor recommends.

There are also treatments like allergen immunotherapy, where injecting small amounts of the symptom-causing allergens on a regular basis trains a person’s immune system over time to not respond to those allergens. Another option is what’s known as sublingual immunotherapy, where allergen-containing tablets or drops are placed under the tongue. This prescription treatment is taken at home and tailored to each patient’s specific allergies by their doctor.

Knowing what you’re allergic to and finding the best treatments will make for a more enjoyable active lifestyle year-round, so it’s helpful to see an allergist to make sure your seasonal allergy survival plan is the right one for you. Allergists can test to identify what is causing the allergic symptoms through either a blood test or a skin test. Allergies can often change over a person’s lifetime. Your allergist can recommend how often you should be tested.
