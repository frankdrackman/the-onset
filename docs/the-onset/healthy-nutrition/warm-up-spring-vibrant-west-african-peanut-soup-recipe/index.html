Warm up to spring with this vibrant West African Peanut Soup recipe

Reviewed by Melissa Rifkin, MS, Montefiore · 2 min read · Prepárese para la primavera con esta exhuberante receta de sopa de cacahuetes del África occidental

In short

As the winter turns to spring, an ideal dish is one that combines the coziness we seek in colder months and the energy-boosting, cleansing and inflammation-reducing ingredients that enliven us in the warmer weather. The perfect balance of those characteristics can be found in this West African Peanut Soup, inspired by renowned food writer Mark Bittman’s recipe, with its protein-filled chicken and nuts, nutrient-heavy sweet potatoes and collard greens, rich in fiber, antioxidants and vitamins. On top of all the health benefits, the colorful, flavorful and textural dish is a fun alternative to more traditional soups, we’re convinced you’ll want to make it again and again!

  • Reviewed by Melissa Rifkin, MS, Montefiore Einstein

The crushed peanuts and peanut butter in the soup combine to give it both crunch and creaminess — not to mention vitamins and minerals. Peanuts boast many qualities including protein, fiber, biotin, copper, niacin, folate, vitamin E, thiamine, phosphorus, magnesium and mono and polyunsaturated fat. This combination of nutrients provides energy, keeps us satiated to reduce cravings for other, less healthy dishes, and can help improve cholesterol levels. To simplify preparation, purchase prechopped unsalted peanuts or buy them whole and break them apart yourself with the side of a knife or the pulse setting on a food processor. Be sure to save ¼ cup to use as a crisp, crunchy garnish to complement the smoother mix of ingredients.

Collard greens are a powerful ingredient from the cruciferous family of vegetables. They're high in fiber; vitamins A and C; iron; and choline, an essential nutrient that can help mood, sleep, muscle movement, and learning and memory functions. Collard greens also contain vitamin K, which helps calcium absorption but can cause negative effects in patients on blood thinners, so be sure to talk to your healthcare team if you’re on these medications.

In terms of taste, collard greens are bitter when consumed by themselves, but, in this recipe, it binds especially well with the sweet potatoes’ saccharine profile. When you combine them with the heartiness of the chicken and tomato and the zest of the cayenne, onion and ginger — which improves circulation, builds immunity and reduces muscle soreness — you get a symphony of fun flavors that sets you up perfectly for an active, vibrant spring season!
