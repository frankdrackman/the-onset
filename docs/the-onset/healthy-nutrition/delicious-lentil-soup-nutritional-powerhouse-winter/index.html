This delicious lentil soup is a nutritional powerhouse for winter

Reviewed by Jessica Shapiro, MS, Montefiore · 2 min read

In short

According to the latest report by the Dietary Guidelines of America, most people in the United States are not meeting the mark when it comes to consuming enough fruits and vegetables. Soups are a great way to sneak in extra vegetables, and this lentil-based dish is a delicious option for getting the nutrients you need in the new year.

  • Reviewed by Jessica Shapiro, MS, Montefiore Einstein

Lentils are really easy to cook, but it’s very important to flavor them. If you try to make lentils and add only a little bit of seasoning, you're going to be disappointed. But this soup has so many elements to it that the main thing you’ll have to worry about is how thick you want to make it.

What’s great about lentils is that they’re a perfect meat substitute and a nutritional powerhouse. I like using them to make meatloaf, burgers and, my favorite, a meat sauce for pasta. The texture is very similar and you’re getting the same amount of protein: a half-cup of cooked lentils gives you 12 grams of protein, which will help with your energy and reduce cravings for less healthy foods.

Another benefit is that, of all the plant-based foods, lentils are the highest in folate, a B vitamin that helps increase the production of red blood cells. Not only is this good for distributing oxygen to all parts of the body, it’s especially good for women who are or are planning to become pregnant, as they’ll need support for the blood-volume increase.

Lentils are also a source of prebiotics, which feed the bacteria in your gut. We all naturally have bacteria that can keep our gastrointestinal system running smoothly or, if we have too much or too little of certain kinds of it, can make us gain or lose weight. You’ve probably seen probiotics advertised in yogurts — those increase the healthy bacteria, and the prebiotics found in lentils are what keeps them going strong.

I always recommend people get more fiber in their diet to help with the GI functions, and this soup has plenty of it. Just be careful — if you’re not someone who’s eaten much fiber, you have to introduce it slowly or it can cause GI distress. If that applies to you, try a smaller portion of this soup as a side instead of a main dish.

As you’ll see in the recipe below, there are a lot of options for fruits and vegetables you can use. When blending it, I recommend doing it in 30-second increments until you get your desired texture — it’s all about personal taste here. No matter what, this lentil soup is going to be a delicious, flavorful way to get so many of the nutrients you need to start off 2021 on a healthy foot. Best of all, you can freeze it for up to four months and it’ll be just as tasty when you reheat it!
