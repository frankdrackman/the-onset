Spice up stay-at-home cooking with this easy, healthy Thai soup

By Jessica Shapiro, MS, CDE, for Montefiore · 2 min read · Alegre su cocina casera con esta sencilla y saludable receta de sopa tailandesa

In short

With dining options limited during the coronavirus pandemic, it’s easy for even the best home cooks to get a little fatigued by their meals. Even if you stocked up on pantry staples and stuffed your freezer full of vegetables and proteins, routinely making the same dishes can get boring, a feeling that only enhances the restlessness most New Yorkers are experiencing while we all stay inside as much as possible.

But sometimes, the addition of one easy-to-find ingredient can truly open up your at-home culinary experiences and bring a bit of excitement to the dishes you’re able to make with what you already have on hand. That’s why for this month, I recommend using curry paste for your next meal and, as an easy way to introduce you to this versatile, affordable ingredient, I’ve included a recipe for a delicious Thai soup.

Curry paste is widely available in most supermarkets and online. There are multiple varieties and brands that offer their own unique blend that may include garlic, ginger, chili peppers, coriander and cumin, as well as other antioxidant-rich spices, so make sure to read the list of ingredients on the packaging and look up any unfamiliar flavoring. For example, if the ingredient list includes a variety of chili peppers then it might be very spicy. One other caveat, as with many premade spice mixes, is that curry pastes tend to be higher in sodium. If there is more than one curry paste available, it may be in your best interest to compare the nutrition labels and opt for the one that is lowest in sodium. Make sure to store the opened container in the refrigerator.

As with any ingredient you’ve never cooked with before, don’t add too much curry paste at the beginning. You could be overwhelmed by the taste, so it’s best to go slowly and keep adding more until you’ve got it just right. It’s also great to pair it with coconut milk, as the combination makes for a much more robust flavor. Coconut milk can be high in fat, so look for a light version — even the lighter coconut milk will be full-bodied enough to make you feel satisfied.

The soup recipe that I am sharing is very adaptable, as are most dishes that involve curry paste. I love to make it using shrimp, but it can work with fish, chicken, beef or tofu. It’s also great with fresh or frozen vegetables — I’ve used portobello mushrooms, peppers, broccoli and spinach. Cook the protein first and set aside. The best way to meld the flavors together is to add the paste once the raw vegetables start to get tender.

In the end, making this Thai soup or adapting a recipe you already know and love with a curry paste twist can really do wonders to break up the doldrums that come with stay-at-home orders, lack of access to our favorite restaurants or the ability to go shopping for a host of ingredients to try cooking something completely new to you. Best of all, if you like it after your first attempt, you can find many variations of these easy-to-use pastes that will let you explore your creative culinary side even further!
