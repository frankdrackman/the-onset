Kids summer menu

By Jessica Shapiro, MS, CDCES. Nutrition Program Manager, Montefiore Einstein Advanced Care · 2 min read

In short

If my family is happy, I am happy. My loved ones relish the occasions when we prepare and dine on a nutritious meal together. Children pay attention to the way the food is presented, the colors, the amount, and, of course, they want their food to be delicious. But, as many parents can attest, it can be a real challenge to satisfy the younger ones in the house.

As easy and playful as food can be.

Childhood is a key stage in terms of dietary education. Younger kids benefit from you making healthy food fun by “playing with the food.” Kids love to help in the kitchen, making them feel extra special to be included in the cooking process. Enlist the help from your kids to prepare these two recipes, ideal for hot summer days: Grilled Watermelon Pizza and Tuna Burgers.

Grilled Watermelon Pizza can be a refreshing summer snack, appetizer, or treat. Watermelon is rich in vitamins, minerals, antioxidants, and electrolytes, which means it can be a great replenisher for the body, especially when it is so hot outside. This can help support your and your kid’s immune systems and overall health. Combining the watermelon with berries, tomatoes, olive oil, and cheese is a mélange of nutrients. Allow the family to have fun with their imagination to create colorful designs and patterns on top of the Watermelon base. Use the suggested toppings in the recipe or be creative and come up with your own!

If your kids like burgers, and when a genuinely phenomenal Tuna Burger is part of the menu, it is a great way to get those omega-3 fatty acids into their diet. Omega-3 is essential; the body needs to get these nutrients from food because it does not produce them by itself. Omega-3s are part of the process of building new cells, the key to developing the cardiovascular and central nervous systems, especially crucial for the growing brains of our kids. A bonus benefit of the fats is that in their presence, they allow the body to absorb other nutrients the body needs from your food. Foods high in Omega-3 include fatty fish (such as tuna), vegetable oils, nuts, ground flaxseeds, flaxseed oil, and leafy vegetables.

As a dedicated dietitian, I am passionate about developing healthy habits and behaviors, especially for our kids. The way you consume food will impact you and your kids in the long run. When you find joy in adequately nourishing and sharing that experience with your family, your kids will learn and appreciate it on many levels. Hoping this summer includes lots of family food “play time” for you and your loved ones.
