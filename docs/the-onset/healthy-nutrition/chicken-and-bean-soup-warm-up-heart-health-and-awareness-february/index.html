Chicken and bean soup to warm up heart health and awareness in

Reviewed by Sandra Arevalo, RD, Montefiore · 2 min read

In short

February, however, is American Hearth Month — the perfect month to take some time to love ourselves through healthy habits. What better way than by eating nutritious foods that help boost heart and circulatory health?

  • Reviewed by Sandra Arevalo, RD, Montefiore Einstein

When we think February and our heart, we may think of love and Valentine’s Day.

Many of us might not associate the food that we eat every day with future health consequences. Instead, we may opt for food that simply tastes good and is convenient, such as fast food. Many of these foods, however, are rich in sugar, sodium and unhealthy fat. The solution? Try incorporating more fruits and vegetables that are high in dietary fiber and vitamins into easy-to-cook meals. This change may be easier than you think.

I helped put together a book of recipes — including the Chicken and Bean Heart Healthy Soup featured below — that require nothing more than a microwave, a few healthy ingredients and basic cooking skills. The project began in 2002 and grew out of Montefiore’s desire to help families who were experiencing housing instability learn how to prepare healthy, affordable and tasty foods without access to a full kitchen. A complete cookbook, titled “Microwave Chef,” is now available online.

All the vegetables make this soup a nutritional powerhouse. You can toss in whatever vegetables you have on hand. Add ground turkey, chicken or lean ground beef for an extra hearty meal with protein. Cut down on the salt with low-sodium bouillon and broth and boost the soup’s flavor with the parsley, Italian seasoning and veggies.

Another major health benefit comes from the soluble dietary fiber in the red kidney beans.

Dietary fiber not only benefits our digestive health, but also benefits our circulatory system. It helps reduce a buildup of plaque in the veins and blood vessels known as atherosclerosis. So, by eating fiber-rich foods, you’re reducing your risk of stroke or heart attack.

Although February can be a cold month that leaves us longing for spring, this recipe can certainly warm up your heart.
