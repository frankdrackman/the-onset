Turkey salad your whole family will gobble up

By Monica Kelly, MS, Healthy Nutrition · 2 min read · La ensalada de pavo que tu familia devorará

In short

Lunch, I feel, is often treated as a bit of an afterthought these days. While it’s not as celebrated as breakfast or as social as dinner, lunch is still just as important for maintaining a healthy lifestyle. One of my biggest challenges has always been finding a reliable option that provides plenty of nutrition, something I can prep in batches to save time in the morning. That's where this incredible lemon-dijon turkey salad comes in.

This meal is the cranberry on top

The main attraction here is using your leftover Thanksgiving turkey. This excellent source of lean protein is rich in plenty of vitamins and minerals, such as B6, B12, Niacin, and iron (which support energy and red blood cell production), as well as magnesium, phosphorus, zinc, and selenium (important for bone health, muscle, and building and supporting a stronger immune system).

The turkey is bolstered by another low-fat, high-protein source: Greek yogurt. Typically, tuna or chicken salad is dressed with mayonnaise, but using Greek yogurt instead instantly cuts down the fat and cholesterol. It’s also a great source of calcium and probiotics, supporting bone health and helping to balance the good bacteria in our GI tract.

The nuts and dried cranberries don't just add colorful, textural variety. Nuts are a great source of heart-healthy unsaturated fats and rich in fiber and antioxidants (they also help keep us fuller for longer). Cranberries also deliver the antioxidant package, boasting plenty of vitamins C and E. Plus, they provide a pop of natural sweetness without relying on refined sugar.

Since I started making this turkey salad, I've saved time, money, and mental energy by having a dependable lunch option that I can make at home. Give it a shot — I'm sure your lunch hour will be transformed just like mine!

1 cup natural plain Greek yogurt with zero percent fat

3 ½ cups of shredded cooked turkey (You can also use ground turkey)

*The % Daily Value (DV) tells you how much a nutrient in a food serving contributes to a daily diet. 2,000 calories a day is used for general nutrition advice.
