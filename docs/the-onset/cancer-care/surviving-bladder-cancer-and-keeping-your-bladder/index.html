Surviving bladder cancer — and keeping your bladder

By Alexander I. Sankin, MD, Associate Residency Program Director, Director of Clinical Trials and Associate Professor of Urology, Montefiore Einstein · 3 min read · Sobrevivir al cáncer de vejiga y preservar el órgano

In short

When a patient is diagnosed with bladder cancer, their first question is often “Will I be able to keep my bladder?” When bladder cancer is caught early, it’s not always necessary to remove the whole bladder. It’s true that for many decades, the go-to treatment for invasive bladder cancer was to remove the bladder, but today we’re doing everything we can to preserve patients’ bladders using a relatively new treatment called “trimodality therapy” — bladder preservation surgery combined with radiation and chemotherapy — that can improve quality of life after bladder cancer.

Bladder preservation is possible with this new approach to treatment.

Like many cancers, bladder cancer is easier to treat if it’s caught early. The first symptom is typically blood in the urine. A urologist might then examine the bladder using a special camera called a cystoscope. A biopsy can show whether any tumor has invaded the bladder wall and help doctors determine the cancer’s stage (how advanced it is) and grade (how abnormal the cells appear), as well as treatment recommendations.

About 70% of new bladder cancers are considered early-stage and can be treated by removing the tumor and sometimes treating with chemotherapy or immunotherapy washings in the bladder. Bladder removal is not necessary to treat these types of cancers.

Patients who have more advanced cancers that have invaded their bladder muscle should talk to their urologist about bladder preservation as a treatment option. Not all urologists offer bladder preservation, and not all health-care systems have the resources needed to coordinate this type of care. When diagnosed with bladder cancer, patients should know that the bladder preservation option exists so they can make informed treatment choices based on discussions with their oncologist.

New treatments help patients avoid bladder removal

While the most common treatment is still often a radical cystectomy — complete bladder removal often preceded by chemotherapy — new bladder preservation treatments have become more popular in the last decade.

Instead of complete removal, the patient undergoes a three-pronged therapy: The urologist removes the tumor through a special cystoscope, after which the patient receives radiation therapy and chemotherapy.

Bladder preservation requires coordinated care and treatments by the patient’s urologist, radiation oncologist, and medical oncologist. Treatments can take four to eight weeks after surgery and might also include chemotherapy and radiation treatments.

Data show that survival outcomes are similar for bladder and bladder removal. In fact, the National Comprehensive Cancer Network guidelines now recommend bladder preservation as an appropriate first-line treatment option for patients with muscle invasive tumors in the bladder.

Can you do anything to prevent bladder cancer?

While there is no proven way to prevent bladder cancer — especially since your age, race, gender, and family history can all be risk factors — there are some smart things you can do to lower your risk:

1. No smoking: Tobacco smoke doesn’t just cause lung cancer. It can also lead to bladder cancer — and is thought to cause half of all cases.

2. Stay hydrated: Drinking lots of fluids, especially water, is thought to lower your risk of bladder cancer.

3. Eat your fruits and veggies: A healthy diet is always a good idea, and eating lots of fruits and vegetables is known to lower the risk of cancer. Studies suggest it can help protect against bladder cancer as well.

4. Avoid exposure to industrial chemicals: Certain organic chemicals, as well as dyes and diesel fumes, put you at higher risk of bladder cancer, so ask your doctor whether any of the chemicals used in your workplace are carcinogenic.

Montefiore Einstein provides state-of-the-art bladder preservation treatments

Montefiore Einstein’s world-renowned urologists provide bladder preservation as a treatment option for patients, and ours is one of the only urology departments in the New York City area that offers all patients with muscle-invasive bladder cancer the option of bladder preservation. We can do this because we have strong collaborations with our colleagues at the Montefiore Einstein Cancer Center.
