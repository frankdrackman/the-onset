Breastfeeding is a personal choice: Know the benefits for baby and mom

By Katharine Kelter, MD, Attending Physician, Comprehensive Family Care Center · 3 min read

In short

Many women know breastfeeding is healthy for babies, but it’s surprisingly good for mom’s health, too. While breastfeeding is a personal choice, knowing all the benefits for baby and mom can help you make the decision that’s right for both of you.

The surprising benefits of breastfeeding

4 things to keep in mind for breastfeeding success

1. Start early: Get off to a good start and ensure a long-term milk supply by latching baby right away after delivery — or as soon as you’re able. Nurse whenever baby is showing hunger cues, at least 8 times in 24 hours. The more you nurse, the more milk you will make. A baby’s stomach is tiny at birth — they need to feed very frequently in the first days and weeks — but this will space out over time as your milk supply increases and their stomach grows.

Know your rights: It is your right to breastfeed anywhere in public, including work, school, restaurants, sports venues, airports, and parks.

2. It should get easier: Know that while breastfeeding may be somewhat uncomfortable and challenging at the beginning, this typically passes in a few days. If pain or discomfort continues, or your baby is having trouble latching, see your doctor or a lactation specialist for an evaluation. Supplement with pumped milk or formula if your baby isn’t taking enough milk at the breast.

3. Pumping as an option: If breastfeeding directly isn’t working out — or you and baby are going to be apart for work, school, or another situation — consider pumping rather than switching to formula. To keep up your milk production, try to pump as often as your baby receives a bottle. Waiting too long can affect your milk production. Breast milk will keep in the refrigerator for three to five days, or in the deep freezer for up to six months. The recently passed PUMP for Nursing Mothers Act protects a woman’s right to pump in the workplace.

4. Know your rights: It is your right to breastfeed anywhere in public, including work, school, restaurants, sports venues, airports, and parks. There is no need to “cover up,” but if you prefer, nursing covers and specialized clothing make it easy to breastfeed anywhere. Many public spaces have private areas for breastfeeding and pumping.

Breastfeeding is not a one-size-fits-all situation; there’s flexibility to tailor the experience to the needs of mother and baby.

Every mother’s decision deserves to be respected

The decision to breastfeed is strictly a personal matter, though friends and family may have strong opinions on the subject. While the American Academy of Pediatrics does recommend exclusive breastfeeding for the first six months — followed by continued breastfeeding as each mother’s situation allows, with the addition of nutritious complementary foods — every mom deserves to be supported whatever her circumstances may be and whatever choice she makes.

More than 80% of newborns start out exclusively breastfeeding. However, that number falls by more than half after three months, when many mothers’ situations change, like going back to work or school, and they start supplementing with formula or switching exclusively to formula. Though formula will meet the baby’s nutritional requirements, it doesn’t provide nursing’s health benefits.

Montefiore Einstein is officially Baby-Friendly

Montefiore Einstein is proud to be designated Baby-Friendly by Baby-Friendly USA, a global program sponsored by the World Health Organization (WHO) and the United Nations Children’s Fund (UNICEF). Montefiore Einstein offers prenatal education on breastfeeding, and our clinic staff are available to provide advice and reassurance on the phone, in person, and using mobile apps. Our doctors, nurses, lactation specialists, and support staff are here to help every mother, whichever choice she makes.
