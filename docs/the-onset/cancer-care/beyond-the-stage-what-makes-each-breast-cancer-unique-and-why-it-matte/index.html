Beyond the stage — what makes each breast cancer unique and why it matters

By Susan Fineberg, MD, Section Head of Breast Pathology and Attending Pathologist at Montefiore Einstein Comprehensive Cancer Center · 4 min read · Por qué cada cáncer de mama es único y por qué importa esa diferencia

In short

A breast cancer diagnosis is often described in terms of stage — a number that signals how advanced the cancer is. But no two breast cancers are alike. Each tumor carries distinct characteristics that affect how it behaves and how it responds to treatment. By identifying these traits, pathologists make it possible to guide treatment beyond the stage and toward more individualized care.

The new information doctors use to advance individualized breast cancer treatment

Breast cancer is defined not only by its stage but also by its own biological fingerprint. Molecular, genetic, and cellular features give each cancer its identity, and this information often matters most when predicting outcomes and guiding therapy. For that reason, pathologists evaluate tumor grade, biomarkers, and type — together with stage — to create a full pathology report that drives more precise treatment decisions. Understanding a tumor’s grade, biomarkers, and type helps doctors develop a treatment plan unique to each cancer. Here’s what each feature tells doctors.

Grade can be used to predict how quickly the cancer is likely to grow and spread. Higher-grade cancers tend to grow and spread more quickly, so doctors may recommend more intensive treatment to manage them.

Biomarkers continue to play an ever greater role in breast cancer pathology as researchers identify new ones, find more advanced ways to test for them, and explore how they can be used to guide treatment.

Biomarkers are proteins or genes that help doctors predict how your cancer will respond to specific treatments like hormone therapy or targeted drugs, allowing them to tailor the most effective approach for your particular cancer.

The type tells doctors where the cancer began and how it behaves.

The new information doctors use to advance individualized treatment

New biomarkers and advanced biomarker analysis. Biomarkers continue to play an ever greater role in breast cancer pathology as researchers identify new ones, find more advanced ways to test for them, and explore how they can be used to guide treatment. One emerging biomarker in breast cancer is the presence of tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes (TILs), immune cells that may be found within a tumor. High TILS, particularly in a triple-negative breast cancer, are associated with improved prognosis and improved response to chemotherapy, including immunotherapy treatment. Immunotherapy helps the immune system target cancer and can offer options to people with advanced or treatment-resistant breast cancer.

Multigene profiling. Multigene profiling — testing that can analyze dozens of genes in a single tissue sample and predict their combined effects on tumor behavior — is one of the most sophisticated tools pathologists use to analyze breast cancer cells today. Backed by advanced genetics technologies like next-generation sequencing, multigene profiling is providing powerful insights into how patients with certain types of breast cancer might respond to treatments like chemotherapy, so the risks and benefits of treatment can be weighed more accurately. It is also used to predict the likelihood of recurrence, helping doctors recommend the most effective long-term treatment plans.

Artificial intelligence (AI). Pathologists are finding new ways to use AI tools that enhance accuracy, improve efficiency, and develop prediction modeling, ranging from rate of progress to reoccurrence and treatment effectiveness. AI technologies can predict genomic alterations and identify key biomarkers in a tumor, as well as help doctors more quickly detect cancer in biopsied tissue and interpret complex biomarker data. The efficiencies enabled by using AI allow pathologists to spend more time on complex cases.

If you need a breast biopsy or surgery, ask your doctor if the pathologists at your facility specialize in breast cancer analysis. It is also helpful to know if your care team participates in tumor boards, where experts meet to discuss and optimize breast cancer treatment for patients under their care. These are helpful in obtaining the most accurate diagnosis and individualized treatment plan possible.
