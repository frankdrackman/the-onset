New advances against leukemia, lymphoma, and other blood cancers

By Amit Verma, MD, Chair, Department of Oncology, Co-Director of the Blood Cancer Institute, for Montefiore Einstein Comprehensive Cancer Center · 5 min read · Nuevos avances contra la leucemia, el linfoma y otros cánceres de la sangre

In short

Blood cancer treatment is entering a new era, bringing powerful breakthroughs that offer renewed hope to people living with leukemia, lymphoma, myeloma, and other blood cancers. Cutting-edge targeted immune therapies and advanced stem cell transplants are not only improving survival rates but also making treatments physically gentler and easier for the body to tolerate. These innovative therapies are transforming lives by giving patients stronger, more effective options than ever before, changing the future of blood cancer care. With these advancements, more patients are living longer, healthier lives and, in some cases, achieving lasting remission.

Innovative blood cancer treatments are giving patients more options & more hope

Chemotherapy advances are driving breakthroughs in blood cancer treatment, with researchers developing more effective therapies that combine different types of chemotherapy and immunotherapy in powerful new ways. Immunotherapy, which helps the body’s immune system identify and attack cancer cells, offers a more targeted approach than traditional treatments. When used alongside chemotherapy, it has led to significantly higher remission rates in certain forms of acute myeloid leukemia (AML), a type of leukemia that has historically been difficult to treat.

A gentler option uses smaller doses of chemotherapy drugs to reduce toxicity, making it better tolerated by patients and often requiring fewer hospital visits, which makes treatment easier to manage overall. This new low-dose chemotherapy approach for AML and myelodysplastic syndromes (MDS) has also shown results comparable to standard treatment plans while causing far fewer side effects.

CAR T-cell therapy (chimeric antigen receptor T-cell therapy) is a revolutionary form of immunotherapy used to treat certain kinds of leukemia, lymphoma, and multiple myeloma. This leading-edge treatment approach involves a person’s own immune cells getting modified to have a heightened ability to identify and destroy cancer cells.CAR T-cell therapy is customized for each individual to eliminate their specific cancer. It can be an option for people whose cancer doesn’t respond to other treatments, or when cancer has returned following a period of remission.

Haploidentical (haplo) stem cell transplants are expanding the donor pool so more people with blood cancers can receive these potentially life-saving treatments. Haploidentical transplants are allogenic transplants, in which a patient’s blood-forming stem cells are replaced with healthy new ones from a donor. While in the past a donor needed to be a perfect biological match, advances now mean people who are only a 50% match can serve as donors. This makes it much faster and easier to find a donor, especially because parents and children are always a half match for one another, while siblings have a 50% chance of being a half match.

Precision medicine breakthroughs are expanding treatment options for people with many types of blood cancer. Precision medicine is a powerful, innovative approach to cancer treatment that uses information about the genetic changes in your cancer cells or tumor to determine the optimal course of treatment. Using new classes of medications, doctors can target specific genetic changes in individual tumors, which can mean more effective results along with reduced harm to surrounding healthy tissue. In addition to targeting the tumor directly, some therapies target the blood vessels feeding the tumor. By limiting the tumor’s “food supply,” precision medicine helps decrease the spread of cancer, making it easier to eliminate specific tumor cells.

New precision therapy drugs such as FLT3 inhibitors and menin inhibitors are being used on their own or in combination with chemotherapy to increase its effectiveness. FLT3 inhibitors target the FLT3 protein—a key driver in the growth of leukemia cells—thereby disrupting the cancer cell’s ability to survive and proliferate. Menin inhibitors interfere with proteins that are critical for the maintenance and expansion of leukemia cells, promoting their death or halting their replication.

Understanding blood cancer risk factors and warning signs

Most blood cancers are caused by a combination of genetic and environmental factors, although some are directly linked to genetic conditions. Children with Down syndrome, for example, are at increased risk of developing leukemia.

Blood cancers can be detected through blood tests, physical exams, imaging tests, and biopsies. Symptoms of blood cancer vary by disease type, and many blood cancers have no obvious symptoms in their earliest stages.

Lymphoma symptoms can include swollen lymph nodes in the neck, armpits, or groin; weight loss; fever; night sweats; coughing, trouble breathing or chest pain; weakness and fatigue; and pain, swelling, or a feeling of fullness in the abdomen.

Leukemia symptoms can include fatigue, fever or night sweats, bruising or bleeding, weight loss or loss of appetite, and petechiae (tiny red dots under the skin).

Myeloma symptoms can include bone pain, often in the back or ribs; bone fractures; weakness or fatigue; unexplained weight loss; frequent infections and fevers; increased thirst; and increased urination.

If you’re experiencing any of these symptoms, talk to a doctor. While they may not always indicate cancer, they can signal other serious health conditions.

Montefiore Einstein Comprehensive Cancer Center is among the elite 1% NCI Comprehensive Cancer Centers in the U.S. and ranked in the top 1% of all U.S. hospitals for cancer care, according to U.S. News & World Report

Researchers at Montefiore Einstein Comprehensive Cancer Center are transforming how blood and bone marrow cancers are treated. They have developed innovative treatment protocols that combine chemotherapy and immunotherapy in new and more effective ways. These breakthroughs are improving outcomes for people with hard-to-treat conditions such as acute myeloid leukemia (AML) and myelodysplastic syndromes (MDS).

Among these advances is a low-dose chemotherapy approach developed by Montefiore Einstein researchers. It offers a gentler, more tolerable treatment option for patients with AML and MDS.

Their research also contributed to the FDA approval of Luspatercept (Reblozyl), the first new treatment for MDS in more than a decade. This marked a major milestone for people living with this complex blood disorder.

Montefiore Einstein Comprehensive Cancer Center is one of only a few institutions nationwide recognized as a Center of Excellence for CAR T-cell therapy by the Foundation for the Accreditation of Cellular Therapy (FACT). It is also home to a national MDS Center of Excellence, staffed by experts dedicated to advancing the understanding and treatment of MDS, AML, and related conditions such as refractory anemia.

With access to more than 500 active clinical trials, our cancer center offers patients cutting-edge cancer therapies that are not widely available elsewhere. These include advanced immunotherapy and precision medicine options that continue to expand what is possible in blood cancer care.
