Low-dose CT scan lung cancer screening saves lives

By Dr. Linda Haramati and Maria Serrano, NP, Montefiore · 4 min read

In short

Lung cancer related to cigarette smoking is a well known public health challenge. What you may not know is that lung cancer takes more lives than breast and prostate cancer combined. Awareness of the benefits of screening for breast and prostate cancer is high, but most people don’t realize that lifesaving screening for lung cancer is now available as well. In fact, only 2%-3% of the eligible population are currently receiving this type of screening, done with a low-dose CT scan that takes less than two minutes.

These state-of-the-art low-dose CT scans are much better than chest X-rays at revealing lung cancer in its earliest stage, when it may be treated and cured. At this stage, lung cancer has no symptoms and is seldom visible on an X-ray. The CT scan is an advanced imaging technology that allows us to view the lungs in extremely fine detail, down to less than a millimeter of thickness in the various airways and vessels. The New England Journal of Medicine published the results of a clinical study in 2011, involving more than 53,000 patients, that compared lung cancer screening using low-dose CT scans and chest X-rays. The lower mortality rate for those screened with CT scans, established that this method saves lives — which is why we began offering these screenings at Montefiore in 2012.

Screening is available based on eligibility guidelines established in the National Lung Screening Trial sponsored by the National Cancer Institute. The guidelines call for annual exams for people ages 55-77, who have at least a 30 pack-year smoking history, which is equivalent to smoking one pack a day for 30 years, two packs a day for 15 years, and so on. Current smokers as well as those who quit less than 15 years ago are eligible. The screening is covered by Medicare, Medicaid and many commercial insurers, or may be paid for out of pocket by anyone who meets the criteria for eligibility.

Unfortunately, it’s not standard practice for many physicians to recommend lung cancer screening in the same way they may encourage patients to have a mammogram or prostate screening every year. This is partly because of the stigma surrounding lung cancer due to its association with cigarette smoking, which patients are often reluctant to discuss. We tell our patients with a history of smoking, “That’s your past. Let’s work on the present and look toward your future.” Both current and former smokers stand to benefit from knowing more about the health of their lungs.

When we speak with patients before their screening, we tell them the discussion about the procedure takes longer than the scan itself. After an explanation about what will happen and how we will proceed if the scan reveals any abnormalities, we talk with current smokers about cessation programs and offer other resources for quitting if they’re interested. Then they change into a gown and lie down on a table. There are no needles, dyes, restrictions on eating, physical contacts, biopsies or a closed tube like in MRIs. The only noise is the whirring of the CT scanner, and the technician’s voice that tells them to take a deep breath and hold it. By the time they can’t hold their breath any more, they’re done and ready to leave. Results are delivered within 24 to 48 hours.

Montefiore's comprehensive support after the screening

If the screening is clear, the patient is reminded to come back in a year. There could be something on the scan that needs further investigation, and in most cases those turn out to be inflammation or something else that will get smaller and go away by the time the patient comes back in a few weeks for another diagnostic test. If there is a result that appears serious and requires immediate attention, patients are scheduled right away with no waiting around for months for an appointment. All of these possible outcomes are explained in advance.

For current smokers, we want to help. We provide guidance on calling 311 and the New York State Smokers Quitline, or using a cessation app. Montefiore offers two programs, free of charge, which are six weeks long, with specialist counseling and the possibility of nicotine replacement therapy like patches, gum or lozenges. There are also medications that can be prescribed. We know quitting can be extremely difficult, and we’re here to offer help and encouragement.

In the past, almost all lung cancers were fatal because by the time they showed up on X-rays, they were already too advanced to cure. Today, lung cancers detected early may have a cure rate of up to 90%. If you meet the criteria, talk with your doctor about scheduling a lung screening with a low-dose CT scan, at a center like Montefiore that is certified by the American College of Radiology to ensure the highest quality. The evidence is there: Lung cancer screening with low-dose CT scans is readily available, and it saves lives.
