This 4-month-old needed a new liver fast. A living donor saved her life.

By Milan Kinkhabwala, MD, Chief of Transplantation Surgery, Director of Abdominal Transplantation, Director of the Hepatobiliary Cancer Program, and Dr. Sarah Bellemare, Surgical Director of Live Donor Liver Transplant and Pediatric Liver Transplant · 4 min read

In short

When we met baby Adriana for the first time, we knew we could save her from liver failure and give her a fighting chance at a normal childhood.

Two moms connected to save baby Adriana’s life

Diagnosed just after she was born with biliary atresia, a rare disease that causes a toxic buildup of bile, Adriana had a liver that was already cirrhotic (impaired by scar tissue), and a previous surgery at another institution meant to prevent further damage had proved unsuccessful. To ensure this 4-month-old would live to see her first birthday, she would need a liver transplant.

But finding a deceased donor would be a challenge. The average waiting time is 86 days for a liver transplant in children, and baby Adriana didn’t have that kind of time. So we made sure her parents knew they would have a better chance if they could find a living donor, someone willing to donate part of their liver to save Adriana. As the liver is the only internal organ that regenerates, using compatible living donors can dramatically cut the waiting times for sick patients.

Having been so closely involved with Adriana’s parents throughout her treatment, it’s impossible for either of us to forget that a parent is entrusting us with the life of their child.

Her desperate parents were unable to donate themselves: her dad, Sal, was not a match, and her mother, Samantha, was ruled out because she had suffered from serious liver problems when she was younger. They quickly spread the word among friends and family and took to Facebook in the hopes of finding a suitable donor.

Luckily, Nicole Killian heard about the baby’s plight. Adriana’s uncle had been invited to a barbecue at Nicole’s house, in rural Pennsylvania, and he told her about his family’s urgent search for a donor. She instantly knew she had to help.

Despite never having met Adriana, and having a 1-year-old daughter herself, Nicole volunteered to donate a piece of her own healthy liver.

Like all organ transplants, live-donor operations are complex. The process demands close cooperation between two surgeons, one of whom meticulously removes organ segments from a donor before the other implants them in the recipient.

On the morning of Adriana’s and Nicole’s operations, we worked in tandem to take care of the patients in separate operating rooms. We removed baby Adriana’s liver and used tiny stitches to attach the piece of Nicole’s liver before replacing the organ in Adriana’s little body. It can easily take six or seven hours to complete transplant surgery on a baby, and the risk of complications such as clotting and twisted blood vessels can be higher than in adults.

Having been so closely involved with Adriana’s parents throughout her treatment, it’s impossible for either of us to forget that a parent is entrusting us with the life of their child.

Thankfully, both Adriana’s and Nicole’s operations went off without a hitch.

Despite the risk of infection and organ rejection, Adriana has recovered rapidly and is now an energetic little girl. Nicole’s liver has completely regenerated itself, and she is back to life as normal.

Ten of 60 transplants we perform at Montefiore are thanks to the overwhelming generosity of living donors. Now we need to push that number higher and ensure that all of our current 1,500 patients in need of a liver or kidney transplant, like Adriana, get to enjoy a future.

Montefiore Einstein: A national leader in transplant and among the highest-ranked in live organ donation

Montefiore Einstein has more than 60 years of experience in adult and pediatric transplants, and our breakthroughs with minimally invasive laparoscopic techniques have made a range of transplant surgeries safer, simpler, and easier on our patients. Our adult and pediatric kidney transplant survival rate is the highest in New York State, with 100% pediatric kidney transplant survival, and our liver transplant survival rate is among the best in the United States, with 100% survival for live-donor liver transplants. Montefiore Einstein also has one of the nation’s longest-running kidney transplant programs, as well as outstanding programs in heart, lung, liver, and pancreas transplantation.

For Montefiore Einstein patients who are waiting for a lifesaving kidney or liver, we created the Live and Let Live Digital Tool to help them tell their “I want to live” personal stories that they can use to create social posts. The tool also creates fliers, posters, and postcards, all with the goal of helping them find a living organ donor.

See the powerful, moving stories of patients who are waiting for a lifesaving organ on our Live and Let Live website and learn more about living organ donation.

If you would like to become a living donor or perhaps need a lifesaving transplant, please visit our Live and Let Live website or call Living Organ Donor–Liver at 1-888-RXLIVER (1-888-795-4837) or Living Organ Donor–Kidney at 1-877-CUREKDNY (1-877-287-3536) to speak with one of our transplant associate specialists.
