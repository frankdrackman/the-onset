Stop and even reverse Type 2 diabetes: New treatments are making it easier

By David J. Carruthers, MD, Internal Medicine–Endocrinology, Diabetes, and Metabolism · 3 min read · Nuevos tratamientos facilitan detener y hasta revertir la diabetes tipo 2

In short

Game-changing new medications called GLP-1 agonists and SGLT2 inhibitors can not only control blood sugar but also help people lose weight by making them feel full faster, so they eat less. Why is weight loss so important? Losing as little as 5% to 10% of body weight can slow and even stop the progression of Type 2 diabetes. Some of these new medications even protect against chronic heart and kidney diseases, which are among the most common and serious complications of diabetes. When combined with a healthy diet and regular physical activity, these new classes of medications are making it easier to slow the progression of Type 2 diabetes — and, in some cases, even stop and reverse it.

New Type 2 diabetes treatments provide game-changing benefits

Why supporting medication with diet and exercise is crucial for controlling Type 2 diabetes

Eating healthy and staying active maximizes the benefits of any medication prescribed for Type 2 diabetes — and is proven to help control the disease. That’s why treatment always begins with a nutrition plan and a program of regular physical activity tailored to an individual’s specific needs, fitness level, and lifestyle.

There’s no one-size-fits-all food plan for preventing diabetes, but a diet centered on vegetables, fruit, whole grains, and lean protein is recommended. The fiber in whole fruits and vegetables can help lower blood cholesterol and glucose levels. It’s also important to avoid added sugars by limiting soft drinks and sweets, as well as refined carbs like white bread, white rice, and white pasta. Whole fruits are a better option than fruit juices or smoothies because smoothies pulverize the fiber naturally present in whole fruit. As a result, blended fruit isn’t nutritionally equivalent to the same fruit left whole, according to some experts.

Regular physical activity is important because it has been shown to improve insulin resistance and lower blood sugar. The American Diabetes Association recommends at least 20 to 25 minutes of moderate exercise (even walking) every day — or 30 minutes a day, five days a week — aiming for a total of 150 minutes a week.

Screening is important for early detection — especially for those at high risk

Type 2 diabetes has no symptoms in its earliest, most treatable stages. That’s why screening tests that measure blood glucose levels are so important. The American Diabetes Association recommends screenings at least every three years beginning at age 35. Testing may need to start earlier for adults and children who are at higher than average risk. Type 2 diabetes is on the rise, but it’s preventable

The number of people with diabetes is expected to double worldwide by 2050, affecting nearly 10% of the population, so taking steps to prevent it is important for everyone. Fortunately, the same diet, exercise, and weight-loss recommendations used to control, stop, and even reverse Type 2 diabetes can be very effective at helping prevent it. Avoiding tobacco products and alcohol can also decrease risk. If a blood sugar screening test indicates prediabetes, the precursor to Type 2 diabetes, adopting diet, weight loss, and exercise recommendations immediately can often reverse the problem, or at least stop it from progressing to prevent diabetes-related organ damage.
