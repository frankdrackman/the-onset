Advancements in obesity treatment make diet & exercise more successful

By Sriram Machineni, MD, Director of the Fleischer Institute Medical Weight Center · 3 min read

In short

Obesity is no longer considered a “weight issue” solved by diet and exercise alone. Today we have revolutionary new treatments empowering people to be successful at losing weight and keeping it off. The upshot is improved well-being and quality of life, as well as reduced risk of medical conditions like type 2 diabetes, heart attack, stroke, dementia, depression, asthma, sleep apnea, erectile dysfunction, and some types of cancers.

New approaches to treating obesity are revolutionizing weight loss

Obesity is a complex — and treatable — metabolic disease, and treating it typically starts with healthy lifestyle habits supported by nutrition counseling, a plan for regular physical activity, and stress and sleep management. If emotional eating or other eating disorders are present, psychological therapy can also help.

Now there’s a breakthrough that can be added to personal obesity treatment plans that is making it easier to achieve and maintain weight loss and reach your health goals. Adding new GLP-1 agonist medications is extremely effective because they work on the brain, not the stomach, to help you feel full and reduce cravings. Some of them may have the additional benefit of increased metabolism and energy.

How to make weight loss and improved health last in the long run

When you’ve tried to lose weight and found it unsustainable, you can work with your doctor to assess your options, including lifestyle changes such as diets that work for you, breakthrough medications, and surgical procedures. Personalized treatment plans can be developed based on detailed assessments, with goals focused on reducing weight and significantly improving health and quality of life.

Diets that work: Extreme calorie-cutting isn’t sustainable. A doctor can help create a more successful plan that focuses instead on strategies that will lead to better health, fitness, and quality of life for years to come: for example, building meals around healthier, more nutritious foods, finding new ways to become more physically active that work for you, and taking medication when it makes sense.

Breakthrough medications: GLP-1 agonists were first developed to help people with type 2 diabetes manage their blood sugar levels, but they’re increasingly prescribed for weight loss as part of a treatment plan that includes lifestyle and diet changes. These medications help people lose weight by reducing hunger and appetite.

Surgical procedures: When other weight loss methods have not worked or if you have serious problems because of your weight, bariatric surgeries like sleeve gastrectomy and gastric bypass — which support weight reduction by modifying the stomach or small intestine — are among your options. These surgeries are safe, minimally invasive, and have helped thousands of people achieve weight reduction for better health. Maintaining a healthy diet and regular exercise habits will help ensure the long-term success of bariatric surgery.
