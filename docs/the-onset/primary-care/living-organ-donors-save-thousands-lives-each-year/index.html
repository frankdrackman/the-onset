Living organ donors save thousands of lives each year

By Gloria Rothenberg, NP, Montefiore-Einstein · 3 min read · La donación en vida de órganos salvan miles de vidas cada año

In short

As of March, 2020 there were 112,000 U.S. residents on the national waiting list for an organ transplant. The overwhelming majority (83%) are waiting for a kidney donation, with another 10% in need of a liver transplant. The rest are waiting for a heart, lung or other organs. Living organ donation is a game-changer, particularly for patients waiting for a kidney or liver donor, and has a huge added benefit in that the living donor saves two lives: the person receiving the organ donation, and the next person on the waiting list. More than one-third of organs transplanted last year — nearly 7,400 — were provided by living donors.

Living organ donation and transplantation came about as a response to the shortage of deceased donors. Although there were nearly 40,000 transplants performed in 2019, another person is added to the waiting list every 10 minutes. Men, women and children of all ages and every ethnicity are hoping every day for that lifesaving donation. If you are considering becoming a living organ donor, you are already among a unique group of individuals.

The Montefiore Einstein Center for Transplantation has more than 60 years of experience, making ours one of the longest running transplant programs in the nation, with one of the earliest living donor kidney programs. With some of the best outcomes in the nation for both kidney and liver transplants, Montefiore-Einstein is home to a multidisciplinary team dedicated to personalized care. We become lifelong partners with both donors and recipients. Most living donors tell us the same thing: They wish they could do it again.

There are some differences between organs from deceased versus living donors. Without taking anything away from the profound lifesaving gift of every deceased organ donation, it is the case that kidneys from living donors start working faster and last longer than kidneys from deceased donors. One of the major benefits is that a recipient does not have to wait on a list for a donor. A patient on the waiting list for a kidney or liver transplant can wait years for an organ from a deceased donor and tragically, about 20 of those waiting are lost each day. The longer the wait, the sicker these patients get, making surgery more difficult for them. While kidney patients can rely on lifesaving dialysis, those needing a liver transplant have no similar option. This is why finding donors is so urgent.

The most common question from potential donors is, “Will I be able to lead a normal life after donating a kidney or part of my liver?” The answer is a very confident “yes.” Once you’ve fully recovered, you’ll have no restrictions aside from our request that you lead a healthy lifestyle. For kidney donors, recovery takes 6-8 weeks; liver donors have a somewhat longer recovery time and must abstain from alcohol for six months to allow their liver to fully regenerate. Female donors can still have children, and everyone can go back to the activities and hobbies they enjoyed before surgery. And, in the very unlikely event you should ever become ill and need an organ transplant yourself, living donors are automatically given top priority on the transplant list. Since the onset of the COVID-19 pandemic, we’ve had inquiries about immune system health after donor surgery. It’s important to know that organ donors are not immune suppressed.

Montefiore-Einstein: Among the best in the nation for donors and recipients

At Montefiore-Einstein, surgeries are performed using minimally invasive laparoscopic techniques for kidney removal, which reduces post-operative pain and scars and shortens recovery time. The surgery for liver donors may be partially laparoscopic as well. Montefiore Einstein Center for Transplantation has among the shortest wait times in the country for kidney transplant and the best liver transplant survival rate in the country, with 100% survival for live donor liver transplants. You’ll find more information, and the living donor forms that are the first step in the process at www.montefiore.org/transplantation.
