"My child fell off the bed and hit their head. What do I do?”

By Andrew Kobets, MD, Assistant Professor of Pediatric Neurosurgery, Montefiore Einstein · 2 min read

In short

It happens. Babies take tumbles and hit their heads. Older siblings smack the newest family member right on their soft spot — accidentally or not. No matter how a baby bumps its head, parents want to know: Is my baby OK? Is it serious enough to go to the emergency room? Here’s what to do when the inevitable happens.

Infant head trauma or just a bump? Know the difference.

Not every fall requires emergency attention. But it’s important that parents know when to take their baby to the emergency room after a bump on the head.

Immediately take a child to the emergency room if they:

If a child is not experiencing any of the above symptoms and seems generally normal, it’s OK to monitor them closely at home for the next few hours. Parents also have the option to book a virtual appointment with their doctor. Symptoms can evolve and change over time, but if the child continues to behave normally, it may be fine to monitor them at home. During this time, check for changes in the size of your baby’s pupils and monitor them while they’re sleeping. If they start vomiting, are unusually tired, or are low-energy — or if their behavior changes or new symptoms appear — take them to the emergency room and tell the doctor the exact circumstances of the injury so that they can better understand what might have happened.

Parents can also check the fontanels, the diamond-shaped soft spots on top of the skull, where the bones of the cranium haven’t yet fused together. A fontanel that is soft, with a sunken compression, is normal. If the fontanel is raised, the baby should be taken to the emergency room, as there may be pressure there from blood or fluid.

Sometimes a parent might not witness or be informed about an incident that could have caused infant head trauma, so it’s important to be aware of the signs and know when to take a child to the emergency room. When in doubt, err on the side of caution.What happens if your baby has suffered head trauma?

If you’ve sought medical attention but the doctor says your baby’s head appears normal, they may still want to observe your child in the emergency room for several hours before releasing you with instructions to see the doctor in a few days or a week. If your baby has a fracture or bleeding of the skull, the doctor may want to keep them overnight for observation.

Most infant head fractures are nondisplaced, meaning the bone is fractured but is still in place. These fractures can heal without further action, and babies are usually sent home the same day after being assessed, once any other neurological issues have been ruled out. Most fractures heal in a few weeks or months, with children rarely suffering developmental neurological damage.

If the doctor’s neurological checks do find damage from head trauma or whiplash injuries, and there’s concern about developmental or cognitive issues in the future, your child will need further testing to determine the most appropriate treatment.
