Tips for dads to be even bigger heroes to their kids

By Traci Maynigo, PsyD, Primary Care · 3 min read

In short

June puts the spotlight on celebrating the importance of dads in the lives of their children, from infancy through adulthood. Fathers make unique contributions to the growth and development of children, regardless of whether they are living in the same household with them. Children are far more likely to thrive when they form strong bonds of attachment with both parents, and when parents can co-parent collaboratively. Many fathers wonder how to be the dads they really want to be, and it’s important to know there is support and guidance available. So, kudos to all the dads we know and love for all they do for their kids! And, if you are looking for ways to up your parenting game even further, here are a few tips, including where to go for help if you need it.

Strong parent-child bonds set the stage for children to develop their own healthy relationships later in life. Research shows that children with involved parents have better relationships with their peers, higher self-esteem and more empathy, and are generally more resilient. No matter what the family structure, children benefit from secure attachments with both parents. Secure attachment is a strong emotional bond in which a child knows they have a secure base and a safe haven with a caregiver. They know no matter where they go, they can always return to that secure base. This grows out of consistency and constancy, as well as accessibility, responsiveness and emotional engagement.

A father’s role goes well beyond being the stereotypical financial provider. The value of his time and attention cannot be underestimated when it comes to forming a secure attachment with a child.

There are many ways for fathers to be involved with their kids, but what’s most crucial is the degree of engagement. Quality trumps quantity when it comes to developing a bond with kids. Those times when we’re distracted, stressed or mentally absent when with our children happen to every parent. Sometimes, just taking a few minutes to let your child know you’re busy, but will be available soon and are looking forward to spending time with them, can make a huge difference in how secure that child feels in the relationship.

For noncustodial parents, making a firm commitment to a consistent schedule of engagement with a child is essential. Making time that is only for them, without distractions, lets your child know you’re emotionally present and strengthens that parental bond with them.

The quality of the relationship between co-parents is important and often related to the degree of the father’s engagement with the child. Fathers who communicate in a healthy, productive and effective way with the child’s co-parent are modeling behaviors that will support that child’s ability to have successful relationships in adulthood.

Conflict between caregivers is inevitable, but it is not necessarily negative for children to see this. How you manage conflict is an important piece of parenting. As a dad, you can demonstrate how to negotiate differences, collaborate and make decisions with others. You can also demonstrate how to repair the relationship after conflict if there are hurt feelings. If they witness the repair, they see that conflict can be productive rather than destructive. Kids learn how to manage their own conflicts by watching how you handle yours, and these lessons can help them be more successful in school, at work and in their adult relationships.

Montefiore Einstein’s nationally recognized support for dads who need it

Montefiore Einstein’s HERO Dads program is centered around strengthening family. HERO stands for Healthy, Empowered, Resilient and Open—qualities that equip fathers to form strong attachment bonds with their children. The program is an outgrowth of Montefiore’s Supporting Healthy Relationships (SHR) program, which has helped thousands of couples improve their parenting and communication skills over the past 15 years. HERO Dads focuses on noncustodial fathers, with added support for employment and financial empowerment. The program helps dads improve their relationship and communication skills, deepen engagement with their children and address financial stressors that conflict with their ability to be the fathers they want to be. Montefiore Einstein’s family-strengthening programs have consistently demonstrated a positive impact. The workshops are facilitated by clinically trained psychologists and mental health counselors, using evidence-based curricula and approaches that have worked for thousands of participants.

If you are a noncustodial father, you may find the support you need from the HERO Dads program. Fatherhood is both profoundly challenging and enormously rewarding. We’re here to help every man be the best dad he can be.

Traci Maynigo, PsyD
