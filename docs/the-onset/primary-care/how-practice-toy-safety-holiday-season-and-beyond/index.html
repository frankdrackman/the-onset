Toy safety tips for holiday gifting: keeping it fun, minimizing the risk

By Meghan Kelly for Montefiore, Primary Care · 3 min read

In short

The holiday gift season can be one of the best times for children, bringing new toys and engaging games that offer opportunities for curiosity, interaction and hours of play. Parents, grandparents, and other adults delight in giving children these fun gifts, though it can be tricky to select a toy or game that is age- and developmentally-appropriate, and most of all safe for the child. Here are some expert tips on how to select and use gifts that bring hours of enjoyment while addressing the need to keep kids safe.

Video games, smartphones, and tablets are sure to top many kids’ wish lists this year. Although they have educational benefits, exposing young children to new skills, they are also taking a toll on the ways children engage with their environments. There is broad agreement that screen time is not the same as face-to-face interaction, and that time spent in front of a screen can affect a child’s language development, gross motor skills, and engagement with their surroundings. Running, jumping, and playing are important to child development, as is interaction with other people and having conversations.

The American Academy of Pediatrics emphasizes the importance of setting limits. They recommend that screens be avoided entirely for children younger than 2, and limiting screen use to one hour of quality programming per day for children from 2 - 5 years old. Co-watching with your youngsters allows them to learn from talking with you about what they’ve seen.

Unwrapping presents is half the fun, but torn wrapping paper and used ribbons be hazards. If left unsupervised, young children can get their hands on small pieces and stick them in their mouths. A simple fix is to use stick-on bows rather than ribbons, and discard the paper after each gift is unwrapped.

Be sure to store them in a dry, clean place to avoid the growth of mold that can make children sick.

Toys need regular cleaning, particularly during cold and flu season when they can host the viruses and germs that cause illness. Parents and caregivers should wipe each toy with an antibacterial or bleach wipe, then rinse with hot, soapy water. Video game controllers, remotes, and iPads should get a regular wipe-down with electronic safe wipes.

Stuffed animals should be stowed during play dates because they are harder to clean. Be sure to store them in a dry, clean place to avoid the growth of mold that can make children sick. Wash stuffed toys regularly to remove dust particles and other allergens.

After outdoor playtime, be sure all the toys are collected and brought indoors. Leaving toys outside is not only hard on the toys, it’s like leaving them in a petri dish where bacteria can flourish. Once everything has been brought inside and put away, make hand-washing the next priority. You can wash up with the kids while singing the ABC’s song, making this into a fun activity that will protect your children from cold-causing germs and nasty bacteria.

The Phoebe H. Helping you protect your children’s well-being is our mission every day of the year. We wish you a safe and healthy holiday season!
