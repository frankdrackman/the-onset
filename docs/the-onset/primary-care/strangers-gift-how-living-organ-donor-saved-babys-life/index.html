A stranger's gift: How a living organ donor saved this baby's life

By Dr. Milan Kinkhabwala, Chief of Transplantation Surgery, Primary Care · 3 min read

In short

When we met baby Adriana for the first time, we knew we could save her from liver failure and give her a fighting chance at a normal childhood.

Diagnosed just after she was born with biliary atresia, a rare disease that causes a toxic buildup of bile, Adriana had a liver that was already cirrhotic (impaired by scar tissue), and a previous surgery at another institution meant to prevent further damage had proved unsuccessful. To ensure this 4-month-old would live to see her first birthday, she would need a liver transplant.

But finding a deceased donor would be a challenge. The average waiting time is 86 days for a liver transplant in children, and baby Adriana didn’t have that kind of time. So we made sure her parents knew they would have a better chance if they could find a living donor — someone willing to donate part of their liver to save Adriana. As the liver is the only internal organ that regenerates, using compatible living donors can dramatically cut the waiting times for sick patients.

Her desperate parents were unable to donate themselves — her dad, Sal, was not a match, and her mother, Samantha, was ruled out because she had suffered from serious liver problems when she was younger. They quickly spread the word among friends and family and took to Facebook in the hope of finding a suitable donor.

Luckily, Nicole Killian heard about the baby’s plight. Adriana’s uncle had been invited to a barbecue at Nicole’s house in rural Pennsylvania, and he told her about his family’s urgent search for a donor. She instantly knew she had to help.

Despite never having met Adriana, and having a 1-year-old daughter herself, Nicole volunteered to donate a piece of her own healthy liver.

Like all organ transplants, live-donor operations are complex. The process demands close cooperation between two surgeons — one of whom meticulously removes organ segments from a donor, before the other implants them in the recipient.

On the morning of Adriana’s and Nicole’s operations, we worked in tandem to take care of the patients in separate operating rooms. We removed Adriana’s liver and used tiny stitches to attach the piece of Nicole’s liver before replacing the organ in Adriana’s little body. It can easily take six or seven hours to complete transplant surgery on a baby, and the risk of complications such as clotting or twisted blood vessels can be higher than in adults.

Having been so closely involved with Adriana’s parents throughout her treatment, it’s impossible for either of us to forget that a parent is entrusting us with the life of their child.

Thankfully, both Adriana’s and Nicole’s operations went off without a hitch.

Despite the risk of infection and organ rejection, Adriana has recovered rapidly and is now an energetic little girl. Nicole’s liver has completely regenerated itself, and she is back to life as normal.

Ten of 60 transplants we perform at Montefiore are thanks to the overwhelming generosity of living donors. Now we need to push that number higher and ensure that all of our current 1,500 patients in need of a liver or kidney transplant, like Adriana, get to enjoy a future.

Montefiore-Einstein: A National Leader in Transplant and Among the Highest Ranked in Live Organ Donation

Montefiore-Einstein has over 60 years of experience in adult and pediatric transplants, and our breakthroughs with minimally invasive laparoscopic techniques have made a range of transplant surgeries safer, simpler and easier on our patients. Our liver transplant survival rate is among the top 10 in the United States, with 100% survival for live-donor liver transplants.

We have one of the nation’s longest-running kidney transplant programs, and among the shortest wait times for a kidney in the country.

Montefiore-Einstein also has outstanding programs in heart, lung and pancreas transplantation. And we are now the only transplantation center with evaluation and consultation locations in Rockland and Westchester counties in New York and Bergen and Morris counties in New Jersey.

A patient’s chance of getting an organ at Montefiore-Einstein is higher than at any other transplant center in the Northeast. Together with the growing number of living donors, we will do even better.

If you would like to become a living donor or perhaps need a lifesaving transplant, please visit https://liveandletlive.montefiore.org/form or call Living Organ Donor Liver at 1-888-RXLIVER (1-888-795-4837) or Living Organ Donor Kidney at 1-877-CUREKDNY (1-877-287-3536) to speak with one of our transplant associate specialists.

Dr. Milan Kinkhabwala, Chief of Transplantation Surgery
