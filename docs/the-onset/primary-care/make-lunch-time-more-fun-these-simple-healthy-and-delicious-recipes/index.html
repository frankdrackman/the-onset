Make lunch time more fun with these simple, healthy and delicious

Reviewed by Sandra Arevalo, RDN, Program Director of Community and Patient Population, Montefiore Nyack · 2 min read · Haciendo más divertido el lunch con estas sencillas, saludables y deliciosas recetas.

In short

In order to succeed in the classroom, students need nutritious lunches to keep them focused throughout the day. However, it’s easy to fall into the trap of convenience over quality when packing a lunchbox. Cold cuts –– even lean cuts –– are usually high in sodium and saturated fat. The good news? There are ways to get creative in the kitchen and whip up tasty school lunches with lots of nutritional value.

Summer is winding down and kids will be headed back to school before we know it.

The two recipes featured below can be prepared ahead of time and stored in the refrigerator for the next day.

The Falafel Pita Sandwiches are good sources of protein, vitamins and minerals. We’re using chickpeas, which are inexpensive and high in protein and fiber. The egg in this recipe adds a boost of vitamin A, calcium and iron. After making the falafel patties, store them in the refrigerator until it’s time for school. Use whole wheat pita bread to serve the patties and low-fat or fat-free yogurt for the Cucumber-Yogurt Sauce.

The Chicken Nuggets recipe is a healthier version of kids’ favorite dish. We’re using skinless, boneless chicken breasts to reduce the amount of fat while still providing an excellent source of protein. By using cereal flakes to create the bread coating instead of flour, we’re incorporating more micronutrients. Check the food label to find a high-fiber, low-sugar cereal, such as bran or corn flakes. Be sure to crush the cereal flakes well, so they stick to the chicken. You can dress the nuggets with a low fat or light dressing to add more flavors with less calories, fat or sugar.

Both the Falafel Pita Sandwiches and Chicken Nuggets recipes are featured in the cookbook titled “Microwave Chef” and can be made with nothing more than a microwave, a few healthy ingredients and basic cooking skills. The cookbook began in 2002 and grew out of Montefiore’s shared collaboration with the Children’s Health Fund to help families who were experiencing housing instability learn how to prepare healthy, affordable and tasty foods without access to a full kitchen.

Discover more ideas for school lunches and learn about Montefiore’s commitment to create a healthy community at www.montefiore.org.
