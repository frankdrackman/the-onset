Flu-proof life: The best defense this season

By Vanessa Pratomo, MD, Family Medicine, Montefiore Medical Group · 3 min read

In short

As another flu season ramps up, getting out in front of it is important for everyone. Those over 65 and children under 5 — especially those younger than 2 — are at a higher risk of complications like ear and sinus infections, pneumonia, and conditions involving the heart, brain, and kidneys. The flu can also be more serious in pregnant women and people with other medical problems like asthma, diabetes, and heart disease. Whether you’re at higher risk of complications or not, here are some ways to flu-proof your life and knock the flu out fast if it strikes.

Ways to fight back as flu season ramps up

The flu vaccine lowers your risk of getting sick and, more important, is proven to be effective at protecting you from serious disease and complications if you do contract the virus. New advice from the CDC this year tells us that egg-free flu vaccine formulations are no longer recommended for people with egg allergies, but you should talk to your provider if you have specific questions about an egg allergy. Seniors over 65 can receive a special high-dose formulation for extra protection. Once you’re vaccinated, it takes about two weeks for your body to build up the antibodies you’ll need for peak protection. The earlier you get a flu shot the better, since flu season typically starts by October and can last until late spring. If you do end up getting the flu, there are ways to minimize symptoms and get you on the road to recovery fast.

How to knock the flu out fast after the first symptoms

Identifying whether or not you have the flu as soon as possible will help your provider determine the treatment that will work best for you. Based on their assessment, which may include a flu test, your provider will decide whether you might benefit from a prescription antiviral medication, which can help your body fight the virus and prevent complications. If you’re experiencing any of these, get plenty of rest and guard against dehydration by drinking plenty of liquids, especially if your symptoms include vomiting or diarrhea. If you don’t see a provider and are experiencing mild flu symptoms, then over-the-counter cold and prescription flu medications can be taken according to directions to relieve symptoms.

Flu viruses spread mainly by droplets when people with the flu cough, sneeze, or talk. Aside from vaccines, good health habits can also stop the spread of flu and other respiratory illnesses. Wash your hands frequently, or use a hand sanitizer with at least 60% alcohol when that’s not possible. Avoid touching your eyes, nose, and mouth, where the flu virus enters the body. You can also add more protection by wearing a mask in public, and sick members of your household can protect others by wearing a mask around others at home.
