Early multiple sclerosis detection & new treatments can prevent disability

By Lauren Gluck, MD, Director, Montefiore Multiple Sclerosis Center · 2 min read · La detección precoz de la esclerosis múltiple y nuevos tratamientos pueden prevenir la discapacidad

In short

The earliest symptoms of multiple sclerosis (MS) can come and go, which makes them easy to ignore — and makes diagnosing this common neurological disease complicated. That’s why it’s so important to know the early warning signs and take the important action of talking to your primary care physician if you experience any symptoms. Treatment options introduced over the past ten years make living a full, active life with multiple sclerosis more possible than ever before, and the newest treatments show great promise when the disease is caught early. Here’s what you need to know.

While there is still no cure for multiple sclerosis, we now have more than 20 FDA-approved medications and therapies, including new, very effective ways to help prevent potentially debilitating new attacks, slow disease progression, and in some cases potentially stop the disease in its tracks. In practical terms, this means people with multiple sclerosis can experience a higher daily quality of life. These treatments can also ward off long-term mobility issues that were nearly impossible to avoid just a decade ago.

Understanding multiple sclerosis and its most common — but often subtle — signs

Multiple sclerosis risk factors and prevention

Risk factors: Multiple sclerosis can affect people at any stage of life, but it’s most often diagnosed between the ages of 20 and 50, and it’s three times more common in women. MS is historically considered most common in white patients, but it is on the rise among Black and Hispanic Americans. Although multiple sclerosis is not directly inherited, the children and siblings of a person with the disease have a greater chance of developing it. Other risk factors include smoking, obesity, and low vitamin D levels.

Prevention: Talk to your primary care doctor if you have a relative with multiple sclerosis and any concerning symptoms. While we still don’t know if there is a way to prevent this disease, it may be possible to lower your risk by maintaining a healthy weight, exercising regularly, and following a healthy eating plan like the Mediterranean diet, which focuses on whole grains, fruits, vegetables, and lean meat or fish. If you smoke, quitting can reduce your risk for multiple sclerosis.
