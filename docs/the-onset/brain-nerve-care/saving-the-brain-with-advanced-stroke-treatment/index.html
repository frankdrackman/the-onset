Saving the brain with advanced stroke treatment

By David J. Altschul, MD, Director of Neurovascular Surgery, Co-Director of Montefiore Comprehensive Stroke Center; Allan Brook, MD, Neuroradiology; Daniel Labovitz, MD, Neurology, Vascular Neurology · 4 min read

In short

Every minute counts when diagnosing and treating a stroke to prevent disability and save lives. In the case of an ischemic stroke, “time is brain,” as we say — since the blood supply to the brain is blocked, depriving it of oxygen. The earlier we can reopen the blocked artery, the better our chances of restoring and preserving brain function for our patients. Newer diagnostic and surgical techniques available at our advanced comprehensive stroke center allow us to work faster, with pinpoint accuracy, and save lives. Here’s what you need to know.

Preserving brain function with smarter, faster technology: Time is brain

The technological advancement that diagnoses and treats stroke with greater speed and accuracy

Advances in computer tomography (CT) imaging — including harnessing the power of AI, or artificial intelligence — now allow us to pinpoint the location of a blockage quickly, assess tissue damage, and immediately alert a doctor with images sent straight to their phone. This makes it possible for a growing number of patients to benefit from thrombectomy, in which we mechanically remove the clot to restore blood flow to the brain. The ability to reverse disability — allowing patients to walk and speak to their families again, regaining their quality of life — inspires our efforts to get stroke patients where they need to be as quickly as possible to receive the treatment they need. Thrombectomy is available only at hospitals that, like Montefiore Einstein, are designated advanced comprehensive stroke centers.

A thrombectomy buys time to prevent disability

With a blood-clot-busting medication called TNK, previously the only proven intervention for acute stroke, treatment must be started less than 4.5 hours after a stroke occurs. While TNK can be a game-changer, many patients arrive too late to benefit from it. However, with mechanical thrombectomies, we are able to treat well beyond this 4.5-hour time limit, with some patients able to be treated up to 24 hours after a stroke. If thrombectomy is needed after treatment with TNK, hospitals that don’t offer the procedure can arrange for transfer to Montefiore Einstein as soon as possible. Based on the latest research, Montefiore Einstein is performing the thrombectomy procedure on a wider range of patients than ever.

A successful thrombectomy enables us to immediately see the signs of reversing a stroke in a patient. Something as small as a patient starting to move their leg again for the first time will have the surgery team cheering, and when the clot comes out, it is truly exhilarating. Having a patient come back to see us after going through rehab and saying, “Thank you so much, I’m back at work,” is what makes us wake up at 3 o’clock in the morning excited to care for our patients.

Even with this wider time window for some patients, every minute still counts. The number-one concern is to get to a hospital without delay: Don’t wait for improvement or advice from a doctor; just call 911.

Recognize the signs of stroke and BE FAST: “Time is brain”

Quick action saves lives, and the acronym BE FAST is a great way to remember the most common symptoms of stroke and what to do if someone, including yourself, is experiencing any of them.

E – Eyes: Have they lost vision in one or both eyes?

F – Face: Does one side of their face droop when they are asked to smile?

A – Arms: Does one arm drift downward when they are asked to raise both arms?

S – Speech: Is their speech slurred when they are asked to repeat a simple phrase?

T – Time: Call 911 immediately if you observe any of these signs

Whenever a stroke is suspected, call 911 immediately. Don’t worry about whether or not your diagnosis is correct. The most important thing is to get to a designated comprehensive stroke center as quickly as possible.

Montefiore Einstein: The first Advanced Comprehensive Stroke Center between Northern Manhattan and Rochester, New York

Individual hospitals have different levels of care available for those experiencing a stroke. The Joint Commission has designated Montefiore Einstein as an advanced comprehensive stroke center (CSC). It’s one of the only CSCs between New York City and Rochester, New York, and one of only about 160 such facilities nationwide. To be certified as a comprehensive stroke center, the center must provide state-of-the-art care for all neurovascular conditions, including stroke, aneurysms, and arteriovenous malformations (AVMs), while following the most current treatment guidelines, achieving outstanding outcomes and leading research to continue to advance effective treatments that give stroke patients the best chance for a full recovery. Montefiore Einstein is proud to have the fastest triage with the best multidisciplinary team working hand in hand across our network, supported by the latest technology, including AI detection.

The Neurovascular Team at the Comprehensive Brain Health Center at Montefiore Einstein Advanced Care, in Westchester, is part of the Comprehensive Stroke Center at Montefiore Einstein. It is led by internationally renowned neurosurgeons, vascular neurologists, neuroradiologists, and rehabilitation specialists who employ the most advanced and emerging imaging technology and diagnostic modalities, along with a spectrum of cutting-edge therapies, including endovascular, open, minimally invasive, and hybrid approaches to treat patients of all ages who suffer from complex, life-threatening neurovascular conditions. Once treated, as soon as patients start improving, they receive state-of-the-art physical therapy and rehabilitation care at Montefiore Einstein’s Burke Rehabilitation Hospital, in Westchester. Studies have shown that this form of multidisciplinary care greatly improves patient outcomes.

We’re among the highest-performing centers in the nation for neurology and neurosurgery, and Montefiore Einstein’s Burke Rehabilitation Hospital is ranked in the top 1% of all U.S. hospitals for rehabilitation, according to U.S. News & World Report. Additionally, we are recipients of the highest standard for rehabilitation medicine, a three-year designation granted by the Commission on Accreditation of Rehabilitation Facilities (CARF).
