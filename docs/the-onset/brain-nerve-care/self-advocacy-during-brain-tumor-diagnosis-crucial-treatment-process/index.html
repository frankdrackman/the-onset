Self-advocacy during a brain tumor diagnosis is crucial in treatment

By Dr. Vijay Agarwal, for Montefiore, Brain & Nerve Care · 2 min read

In short

While a person is not able to control the risk factors that increase their chance of developing a brain tumor, such as age, genetics and family history, understanding where to turn after receiving a diagnosis can help remove some of the uncertainty of receiving a brain tumor diagnosis.

With new research, technology and medical advancements, brain tumors are becoming more treatable than ever before, and many are curable.

When a brain tumor develops, it is a result of a growth of abnormal cells growing in the brain and can be both cancerous (malignant) and non-cancerous. It can impact a person’s ability to function normally. The good news? Most brain tumors are not cancer; less than one-third of all diagnosed tumors in the U.S. are cancerous. In addition, there is an entire category of generally slow-growing brain tumors, known as skull-base tumors, that, if treated by a specialized team, are associated with long-term survival.

A person’s skull base is the area behind their eyes and nose that slopes down to the back of the head, forming at the base of a skull. When referencing a skull-base tumor, it entails an abnormal growth developing in this specific, difficult-to-reach location. Generally, the signs of this growth occur when important nerves or arteries are affected, and the symptoms can vary from person to person. A CT scan and an MRI can help determine the exact nature of the growth, and specialized scans of the patient’s brain are used to create a map to accurately navigate around important structures.

There is a common misconception that a brain tumor diagnosis is equivalent to a death sentence. This is not the case. With the right experience and expertise, neurosurgeons can accurately diagnose and treat brain tumors with minimally invasive surgery or a combination of minimally invasive surgery and more traditional open surgeries, resulting in a shorter-than-average recovery time for many individuals and more complete removal of the tumor.

For skull-base tumors, its deep location in the brain and proximity to important arteries and nerves can present significant challenges when operating. When seeking surgical treatment for this type of tumor, it is important to evaluate doctors to ensure they have a comprehensive medical team, such as Montefiore’s neurological surgery team, that can take a multidisciplinary approach to minimally invasive surgery. Having a range of disciplines is important and can contribute to a faster recovery. Choosing a neurosurgeon with advanced training in skull base is also very important.

A brain tumor diagnosis and treatment can feel overwhelming and complicated — more reason than ever to become your own advocate. When you feel empowered to communicate the symptoms most concerning to you, it can help doctors understand the best approach to treatment.
