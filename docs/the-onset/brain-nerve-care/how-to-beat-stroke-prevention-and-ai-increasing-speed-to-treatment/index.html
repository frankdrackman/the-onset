How to beat stroke: Prevention and AI increasing speed to treatment

By David J. Altschul, MD, Director of Neurovascular Surgery and Co-Director of the Montefiore Comprehensive Stroke Center · 4 min read

In short

The risk of stroke increases with age, but over the past two decades, strokes have been increasing among people under 50. While many strokes can be prevented, if a stroke does happen, new AI-assisted technologies support faster, more accurate diagnoses, leading to expanded options for treatments that begin more quickly and help preserve brain function. Here’s what to know about beating — and preventing — strokes.

Up to 80% of strokes are preventable. But once a stroke happens, speed to care is critical.

Through health screenings, medication to treat preexisting conditions, and lifestyle changes, most strokes can be prevented. It’s never too early, or too late, to start.High blood pressure is the biggest risk factor for stroke. It’s known as the silent killer because people may have it for years without experiencing symptoms or seeking treatment ... until they have a stroke.

Other risk factors include high cholesterol, diabetes (high blood sugar), carotid artery disease, atrial fibrillation (AFib), obesity, and smoking or vaping.

Quick action saves lives, and the acronym BE FAST is a great way to remember the most common symptoms of stroke and what to do if someone, including yourself, is experiencing any of them.

Good lifestyle choices like tobacco cessation, getting routine exercise, and following a healthy diet, with a low intake of salt and simple carbohydrates, are important for curbing stroke risk.

Women can be at a higher risk of stroke during the third trimester of pregnancy and right after they give birth, especially if they have preeclampsia (a pregnancy complication characterized by high blood pressure). Women who use oral contraceptives are also at a slightly higher risk of stroke, especially if they also smoke.

AI is advancing technologies that mean faster diagnosis and expanded options for better treatment

A person having a stroke loses neurological function due to blocked blood vessels in the brain, a brain bleed, or a ruptured brain aneurysm. Before doctors can help, they need to assess exactly what’s occurred and how much brain tissue has been affected. That’s happening much more quickly today, thanks in part to artificial intelligence (AI) powered computer tomography (CT) imaging that can pinpoint the location of a blockage, assess tissue damage, and immediately alert a doctor with images sent straight to their phone.

Faster and more detailed diagnoses are also making it possible for more people to benefit from thrombectomy, a procedure that can immediately reverse stroke disability and is offered only at a certified comprehensive stroke center. Montefiore Einstein is one of just 170 facilities in the country that the Joint Commission has designated an advanced comprehensive stroke center, providing the highest level of care available.

Recognize the signs of stroke and BE FAST: Time is brain

Quick action saves lives, and the acronym BE FAST is a great way to remember the most common symptoms of stroke and what to do if someone, including yourself, is experiencing any of them.

E – Eyes: Have they lost vision in one or both eyes?

F – Face: Does one side of their face droop when they’re asked to smile?

A – Arms: Does one arm drift downward when they’re asked to raise both arms?

S – Speech: Is their speech slurred when they’re asked to repeat a simple phrase?

T – Time: Call 911 immediately if you observe any one of these signs

Whenever a stroke is suspected, call 911 immediately. Don’t worry about whether or not your diagnosis is correct. The most important thing is to get to a certified comprehensive stroke center as quickly as possible.

Montefiore Einstein: The first Advanced Comprehensive Stroke Center between Northern Manhattan and Rochester, NY

Individual hospitals have different levels of care available for those experiencing a stroke. The Joint Commission has designated Montefiore Einstein an advanced comprehensive stroke center (CSC), providing the highest level of care available — the first CSC between New York City and Rochester, NY, and one of only 170 such facilities nationwide. To be certified as a comprehensive stroke center, the center must provide state-of-the-art care for all neurovascular conditions, including stroke, aneurysms, and arteriovenous malformations (AVMs), while following the most current treatment guidelines, achieving outstanding outcomes, and leading research to continue to advance effective treatments that give stroke patients the best chance for a full recovery.

Montefiore Einstein is proud to have the fastest triage with the best multidisciplinary team working hand in hand, across our network, supported by the latest technology, including AI detection. Once treated, as soon as patients start improving, they receive state-of-the-art physical therapy and rehabilitation care at Montefiore Einstein’s Burke Rehabilitation Hospital, in Westchester. Studies have shown that this form of multidisciplinary patient care greatly improves patient outcomes. That is why our center has been recognized by the American Heart Association as an elite stroke hospital for meeting and exceeding key benchmarks related to patient care.

At Montefiore Einstein, when a patient needs stroke care, we bring all of our skills together to give them the best treatment available, working as a patient-centered team.
