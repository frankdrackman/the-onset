Your at-home defense against stroke

By Charles C. Esenwa, MD, Director of the Center for Comprehensive Stroke Care · 4 min read · Su defensa en casa ante un accidente cerebrovascular

In short

There are 8 surprisingly powerful habits you can do at home to cut your risk of stroke by as much as 80%, regardless of your age — increasing your odds of saving brain cells and reversing the potential for long-term disability. With one in six people today suffering a stroke, it’s more important than ever to beat the odds.

Beat the odds of having a stroke with these 8 habits

Here are the 8 habits you can do at home to beat the odds of having a stroke and what to know if stroke strikes.

1. Controlling high blood pressure: High blood pressure is the biggest risk factor for strokes. We call it the silent killer because people may have high blood pressure for years without experiencing any symptoms until they have a stroke. It’s important to get your blood pressure checked regularly, even if you’re under 50.

If doctors know a patient’s risk, they can help lower it with recommended lifestyle modifications and medications.

2. Paying attention to your heart and underlying conditions: Treating conditions like atrial fibrillation (AFib), heart disease, and carotid artery disease reduces your risk of having a stroke.

3. Eating a low-sugar/low-sodium diet: Consume a diet rich in fruits and vegetables and reduce sugar and salt intake. The American Heart Association recommends a Mediterranean-style diet — one focused on whole grains, fruits, vegetables, nuts and legumes, fish, and healthy fats like olive oil — to reduce stroke risk and support cardiovascular health. Kick-start your healthy habits today with delicious mini garden-fresh vegetable egg bites.

4. Engaging in regular physical activity: The American Heart Association recommends 150 minutes of physical activity per week (or 30 minutes a day, five days a week) for adults and at least 60 minutes of exercise per day for kids ages 6 to 17.

5. Quitting smoking or vaping: Tobacco use can damage blood vessels, increase heart rate, cause inflammation, and make blood more likely to clot, all of which increase stroke risk.

6. Limiting stress: Stress can lead to increased blood pressure and higher levels of sugar and fat in the blood, which can increase the risk of clotting.

7. Screening for/managing high cholesterol and diabetes: Regular screening for high cholesterol and diabetes (high blood sugar) is important, especially if they run in the family. High cholesterol can lead to prediabetes and type 2 diabetes, which raises stroke risk.

8. Getting healthy sleep: Adequate sleep promotes healing, improves brain function, and reduces the risk of stroke and many chronic diseases. Adults should seek 7 to 9 hours of sleep each night.

If doctors know a patient’s risk, they can help lower it with recommended lifestyle modifications and medications. It’s important for women to know that they can be at a higher risk of stroke during the third trimester of pregnancy and right after birth, especially if they’ve been diagnosed with preeclampsia, a pregnancy complication characterized by high blood pressure. Women who use oral contraceptives are also at a slightly higher risk of stroke, especially if they also smoke.

Recognize the signs of stroke and BE FAST: “Time is brain”

Quick action saves lives, and the acronym BE FAST is a great way to remember the most common symptoms of stroke and what to do if someone, including yourself, is experiencing any of them.

E – Eyes: Have they lost vision in one or both eyes?

F – Face: Does one side of their face droop when they’re asked to smile?

A – Arms: Does one arm drift downward when they’re asked to raise both arms?

S – Speech: Is their speech slurred when they’re asked to repeat a simple phrase?

T – Time: Call 911 immediately if you observe any one of these signs

Whenever a stroke is suspected, call 911 immediately. Don’t worry about whether or not your diagnosis is correct. The most important thing is to get to a certified comprehensive stroke center as quickly as possible.

Montefiore Einstein: The first designated Advanced Comprehensive Stroke Center between Northern Manhattan and Rochester, New York

Individual hospitals have different levels of care available for those experiencing a stroke. The Joint Commission has designated Montefiore Einstein as an advanced comprehensive stroke center (CSC). It’s one of the only CSCs between New York City and Rochester, New York, and one of only about 300 such facilities nationwide. To be certified as a comprehensive stroke center, the center must provide state-of-the-art care for all neurovascular conditions, including stroke, aneurysms, and arteriovenous malformations (AVMs), while following the most current treatment guidelines, achieving outstanding outcomes, and leading research to continue to advance effective treatments that give stroke patients the best chance for a full recovery. Our world-renowned doctors use the latest technology and techniques to diagnose and treat stroke faster and more accurately than ever before, increasing the odds of reversing long-term disability.

Once treated, as soon as patients start improving, they receive state-of-the-art physical therapy and rehabilitation care at Montefiore Einstein’s Burke Rehabilitation Hospital, in Westchester. Studies have shown that this form of multidisciplinary care greatly improves patient outcomes.

We’re ranked in the top 1% of all U.S. hospitals for neurology and neurosurgery, and Montefiore Einstein’s Burke Rehabilitation Hospital is also ranked in the top 1% of all U.S. hospitals for rehabilitation, according to U.S. News & World Report. Additionally, we are recipients of the highest standard for rehabilitation medicine, a three-year designation granted by the Commission on Accreditation of Rehabilitation Facilities (CARF).
