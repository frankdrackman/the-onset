How to spot heart failure — plus the latest life-changing treatments

By Yogita Mahendra Rochlani, MD, Assistant Professor of Medicine—Cardiology · 4 min read · Insuficiencia cardiaca: detección, y nuevos y transformadores tratamientos

In short

For millions of people with heart failure, the latest treatment options — including advanced surgical procedures and innovative devices to assist a failing heart — can be game-changing, especially when paired with a healthy lifestyle. Not only are we able to alleviate symptoms, but we can also help people with heart failure live longer, fuller lives. Starting treatment as soon as possible is essential, so beyond learning about the latest advances, it’s also important to understand your risk of heart failure and how to spot the signs. Here’s what you need to know.

Warning signs of heart failure and why early treatment is key

Revolutionary new treatments for heart failure are extending life

Twenty years ago, people with advanced heart failure often had limited options and a much lower chance of surviving even one year. Today, that’s no longer the case. While diet and healthy habits are the foundation of heart failure treatment, people are benefitting from exciting new surgical procedures, devices, and new classes of medication originally developed for diabetes, obesity, and high blood pressure — all key risk factors for heart failure.

Mitral valve clips: These tiny clips are one of the latest technology advances used to repair a leaky mitral valve, which can make heart failure worse. It’s done without open-heart surgery.

ICDs: Implantable cardiac defibrillators are tiny devices a heart surgeon can place in the chest to prevent dangerous heart rhythms that could lead to cardiac arrest, which can be a complication of heart failure.

Ablation: This technique involves “zapping” tissue in the heart and is used to treat abnormal heart rhythms, like atrial fibrillation, that can worsen heart failure symptoms.

GLP-1 agonists: These medications, known for helping people with diabetes and weight loss, are also being studied for their effects on heart health. Early research shows they may help reduce heart risks, especially in people with obesity.

SGLT2 inhibitors: Originally for diabetes, these medications have become a game-changer for people with heart failure, improving heart and kidney function — even for those without diabetes.

ARNIs: These medications help control blood pressure by preventing blood vessels from contracting and are replacing ACE inhibitors because they’re more than 20% more effective at reducing not only the risk of hospitalization but also heart-failure-related death.

Why treating heart failure early is so important

Because heart failure is the result of the heart’s inability to pump blood efficiently, receiving treatment as soon as possible is essential. Without treatment, the body’s organs may not be receiving the blood they need, which can lead to complications like kidney problems or make existing conditions like chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) or liver disease worse.

Healthy lifestyle habits that can slow the progression of heart failure

If you are at risk or have already experienced heart failure, there are steps you can take to optimize care and slow the progression of heart failure. Even small changes — like walking daily, cutting back on salt, or quitting smoking — can make a big difference.

Making healthy lifestyle choices and maintaining overall health are crucial components of any heart failure treatment plan. Talk to your doctor about creating the plan that will work best for you.
