James's heart-failure reversal is one for the record books

By Ulrich P. Jorde, MD, Head of Heart Failure, Cardiac Transplantation, and Mechanical Circulatory Support · 4 min read

In short

When James Bradley recounts his incredible journey to me, he still can’t believe he survived. As James tells it, one muggy summer day, as he reported for duty as White Plains deputy commissioner of public safety, he realized something in his chest didn’t feel quite right. As James recalls, “Everybody always told me that when you have a heart attack it feels like an elephant is sitting on you. I didn’t have that.”

Saving James without a heart transplant

Hours later he suffered a second heart attack.

The 68-year-old had rarely taken a sick day since he joined the force as a beat cop in the 1970s. It was only when his wife, Robin, convinced him to seek help that he drove himself to urgent care. Doctors there immediately realized he was having a heart attack. Despite the doctors’ best efforts to increase blood flow to his heart, cardiologists were unable to stop him from having another massive heart attack just hours later.

They called me from the hospital that night to see if we could save his life. By the time James arrived at Montefiore Einstein, he was in grave danger.

Early the next morning, he suffered a series of cardiac arrests requiring multiple resuscitations and electrical shocks to restore his heart rhythm. As one of a few centers that uses ECMO (extracorporeal membrane oxygenation), the highest level of life support, we managed to stabilize James using this technology to pump and oxygenate blood outside the body in order to give his heart a rest. But this was only a short-term bridge to a definitive heart-replacement therapy like a durable left ventricular assist device (LVAD) or heart transplant. By now, he was in an induced coma. His closest friends had driven down to be with his terrified wife, who called a Catholic priest to administer last rites at his bedside.

Over the next few days, cardiothoracic and vascular surgeon Dr. Daniel Goldstein and I closely monitored James’s condition as he lay in intensive care. We ruled out waiting for a potential heart transplant and decided his best shot at survival would be an LVAD, which would assist James’s heart muscle in pumping blood throughout his body.

The next 24 hours were critical, but once it was clear the LVAD was stable, we knew the grandfather of five would recover.

As someone used to juggling teams of people and negotiating difficult situations, James was determined to conquer his recovery process, which made him a perfect partner with his physical and occupational therapy team. He and his team at Montefiore Einstein’s Burke Rehabilitation Center, in Westchester, ensured that the deputy commissioner of public safety was able to get back to doing what he loves: serving his community.

Incredibly, James’s test results showed that his heart function had also returned to near normal, and — one year after his heart attacks — we took the rare step of removing the LVAD.

Two years later, James has retired and is loving spending time with Robin and overindulging their clutch of grandkids. They’ve bought a giant SUV and dream of renting a string of beach houses one summer for vacationing with their family.

He says he has been so lucky, he just wishes he had played the lottery.

Montefiore Einstein’s Center for Heart and Vascular care is ranked in the top 1% of all hospitals in the nation for cardiology and cardiothoracic surgery by U.S. News & World Report

Montefiore Einstein’s nationally recognized Center for Heart and Vascular Care has also achieved a “high performing” ranking in heart failure from U.S. News & World Report. The Center for Heart and Vascular Care is a leader in heart rhythm disorders, as well as interventional and minimally invasive cardiac procedures, and is at the forefront of using ventricular assist devices (VADs) for the treatment of heart failure.

The Montefiore Einstein Center for Heart and Vascular Care is an international referral site for heart failure, arrhythmia, and mechanical circulatory support, including procedures such as cardiac-assist device implantation and catheter ablation, performing more than 1,300 ablation procedures annually. Our center also serves as a rigorous educational facility for medical fellows, taking great pride in training the next generation of innovative cardiologists.

Montefiore Einstein continues to expand our cardiac surgery program across the region. In just one year of the opening of the Montefiore Einstein Cardiac Surgery Program at White Plains Hospital, in November 2021, more than 100 hearts and lives have been saved.

Montefiore Einstein is restoring lives through innovative heart care for our patients every day. Our unique patient care model provides comprehensive, individualized treatment and leverages the vast resources available to our specialists, including groundbreaking proprietary research and state-of-the-art diagnostic and treatment technology. Developing the first transvenous cardiac pacemaker and performing the first coronary bypass operation was only the beginning of Montefiore Einstein’s more than 60 years of leadership in heart care — and we’re not slowing down.
