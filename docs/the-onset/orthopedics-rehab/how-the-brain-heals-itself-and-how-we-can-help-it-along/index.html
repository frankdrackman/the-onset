How the brain heals itself and how we can help it along

By Sharon Bushi, MD, Attending Physician at Burke Rehabilitation Hospital · 2 min read · Cómo se cura el cerebro a sí mismo y cómo favorecer la sanación

In short

Not long ago, a stroke or traumatic brain injury meant learning to live with permanent loss. Today, it means something very different: a real chance at regaining movement, speech, memory — and hope. Thanks to breakthroughs in neuroscience and new rehab technologies, recovery is no longer about settling but about striving.

Neuroplasticity lets the brain adapt and rewire—and therapies make recovery even stronger.

What makes this new era of recovery possible is the brain’s own ability to adapt — what scientists call neuroplasticity. With the right stimulation and support, even an injured brain can form new connections and relearn lost skills. From cutting-edge robotics and virtual reality to personalized therapy plans and AI-driven progress tracking, today’s rehabilitation tools are more precise, more motivating, and more effective than ever. The goal isn’t just to cope — it’s to come back stronger.

Harnessing neuroplasticity, the brain’s self-healing superpower

The proven therapies that form the core of recovery — physical, speech, cognitive, psychological, and occupational — are still essential.

Today, doctors can observe neural activity better than ever. This has transformed our understanding of how the brain uses neuroplasticity to heal — naturally making new connections and rewiring itself after a stroke or TBI. Though injured brains are always working to restore lost function, neuroplasticity is especially active in the first few months. To take advantage of this window of opportunity and boost the chances of a faster and more complete recovery, doctors leverage state-of-the-art technologies and therapies like robotics, virtual reality, and breakthroughs in nerve stimulation.

The proven therapies that form the core of recovery — physical, speech, cognitive, psychological, and occupational — are still essential. What’s new is how we’re using technology in smarter, more innovative ways to strengthen those methods, helping patients regain function more fully than we once thought possible — and, in some cases, recover abilities once believed to be gone for good.

Every person’s brain — and every recovery — is unique. But what unites all survivors is the remarkable resilience of the human mind. If you or a loved one are navigating life after a brain injury, know this: your brain is capable of more than you think, and the right care can help bring that potential to life.

Montefiore Einstein is an internationally recognized leader in the fields of restorative neurology and neurorehabilitation

Montefiore Einstein is dedicated to improving and restoring residual neurological function in adults and children with nervous system disorders. Our multidisciplinary team of world-renowned specialists brings together research and clinical care, advancing the treatment of the full range of pediatric and adult nervous system disorders, including stroke and associated neurovascular conditions; multiple sclerosis and other demyelinating conditions; Parkinson’s disease and other movement disorders, including dystonias, neuromuscular disorders, and neurodegenerative diseases; pediatric and adult cognitive disorders; concussions and traumatic brain injuries; paralysis and spinal cord injuries; as well as the full spectrum of other nervous system conditions.

We are ranked in the top 1% of all hospitals in the nation for Neurology and Neurosurgery, and Montefiore Einstein’s Burke Rehabilitation Hospital is ranked in the top one percent of all U.S. hospitals for rehabilitation, according to U.S. News & World Report. Additionally, we are recipients of the highest standard for rehabilitation medicine, a three-year designation granted by the Commission on Accreditation of Rehabilitation Facilities (CARF).
