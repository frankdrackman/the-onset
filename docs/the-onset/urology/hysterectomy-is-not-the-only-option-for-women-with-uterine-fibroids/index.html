Hysterectomy is not the only option for women with uterine fibroids

By Jacob Cynamon, Director of the Division of Vascular and Interventional Radiology, Montefiore Einstein · 3 min read · La histerectomía no es la única opción para mujeres con fibromas uterinos

In short

Many women, at some point in their lives, will experience one or more of the symptoms related to uterine fibroids, including heavy menstrual bleeding, bloating, back pain, and pressure on their bladder. While fibroids are benign, not cancerous growths, they can make life uncomfortable.

The uterine fibroid treatment option women should know about

Many women believe that the only way to treat fibroids is with a hysterectomy, which is removal of the entire uterus. But today we can eliminate fibroids with what’s known as uterine fibroid embolization, or UFE. This quick, effective treatment is minimally invasive and allows women to avoid hysterectomy surgery.

Fibroids are areas of overgrown tissue on the muscular wall of the uterus that usually occur when women are in their 30s and 40s. While these growths may be abnormal, they’re also extremely common, affecting up to 70% of women. Studies have shown that Black women tend to develop fibroids at a slightly younger age than women of other races, and their lifetime prevalence is higher: around 80%. There are no definitive causes, but genetics may play a role. Clinically significant fibroids — those that can cause bleeding or pain — can be as small as a grape or as large as a grapefruit, and the size can play a role in whether a woman experiences symptoms.

You don’t have to live with the discomfort of fibroids

Women who experience fibroid symptoms do not have to live with them. So many women have seen a mother or an aunt deal with similar discomfort and just assume it is normal. They may have also seen their older relatives undergo hysterectomies to manage fibroids and might be afraid to seek out treatment. But for the past 20 years, we’ve found that UFE is usually the best first treatment option. If UFE doesn’t work, a hysterectomy may be considered down the line, but it’s no longer our go-to treatment.

UFE is a good option for women who are experiencing uncomfortable fibroid symptoms and want to avoid surgery. While UFE can lead to improved fertility if the fibroid was blocking implantation, the treatment may carry some fertility risks, as well as risk of early menopause, though the latter is rare. If you’re planning on getting pregnant in the future, talk to your doctor about whether or not UFE is right for you.

Doctors can often discover fibroids in a routine gynecological exam. They then may recommend an ultrasound or an MRI in order to get a clearer picture of the size of the fibroids and whether they require treatment. If the fibroids are not causing significant symptoms, doctors will likely simply continue to monitor the patient.

Discomfort during the UFE procedure is minimal. The post-procedure pain related to the embolization of the fibroids can be managed expertly by post-procedure nurses. Patients can usually go home a few hours after the procedure. Most women are up and around in just a few days.

If UFE is not provided to you as an option for fibroid treatment, consider seeking a second opinion, as hysterectomy may not be your only solution.

Montefiore Einstein: a pioneer in uterine fibroid treatment

Montefiore Einstein’s Fibroid Center has more than 25 years of experience with uterine fibroid embolization. Our radiologists pioneered the use of cone-beam CT to refine the accuracy of the treatment and reduce radiation exposure, and our department has been designated a Diagnostic Imaging Center of Excellence by the American College of Radiology. Our team works closely with women’s gynecologists to provide a seamless care experience and is staffed with nurses specializing in post-procedure recovery.
