Male sexual dysfunction: A normal part of aging, or is it an important

By Dr. Pedro Maria, D.O., Urology · 5 min read

In short

June brings National Men’s Health Month, with its focus on building awareness of male health issues. An important aspect of a man's health that motivates him to see a doctor is a urological condition, even though many men are uncomfortable or embarrassed to talk about issues like erectile dysfunction (ED). According to the American Urological Association, as many as 30 million men in the U.S. suffer from erectile dysfunction and many incorrectly believe it is a normal part of aging. But ED often signals a more serious condition that should not be ignored.

Sexual dysfunction can be detrimental to relationships as well, so it’s vital for a man’s overall well-being to find out and treat what’s causing it. Understanding ED, including how to prevent it and what to expect when seeking care from a doctor, is important for every man’s health.

Sexual dysfunction may be sending you an important message about your health

It used to be thought that ED was largely a psychological issue, but that’s a myth. In fact, 75% to 90% of patients with the condition have an anatomical or physiological cause. Yes, there may be occasions when your mood is the issue, but it passes and everything gets back to normal the next time. In that case, you don’t need to be concerned about it. But when it happens once, then the next week, then again the following month, and it’s happening more often, it’s time to make an appointment with the doctor.

Because the penis is a vascular organ, conditions that affect your arteries and veins can impact your erection.

Men seeking care for ED may learn they have high cholesterol, hypertension, diabetes, a sexually transmitted disease or prostate problems. These are serious conditions that respond well to treatment in most cases, and early diagnosis leads to better outcomes. This is particularly important for younger men, in their 40s to 60s, where ED can be an early sign of heart or vascular disease. This is why it’s so important to have a conversation with your doctor as soon as you notice a problem.

There are a number of things you can do to prevent erectile dysfunction:

There is a lot of misunderstanding about over-the-counter remedies that promise to cure impotence. Because they can't address the underlying causes of ED, they're not the answer. There are also some myths about the role of testosterone in sexual function. While this hormone has a part to play, if you don’t have an erection, testosterone won’t give you one. There is a complex relationship between testosterone and metabolic health, which is currently being studied, and your doctor is the best source of information on whether or how it will help you.

The most important thing to remember when you see the doctor is to be completely honest about what you are experiencing. Up to 40% of men who visit a urologist don’t mention that they have issues with ED unless the doctor asks about it, so be proactive and bring it up at your appointment if you have concerns. Also consider bringing your spouse or significant other into the conversation about diagnosis and treatment. Many times, a partner offers a unique perspective that provides the doctor more insight. It’s helpful to have everyone at the table.

At the first visit, your urologist will want get a clear picture of your overall health. Because the penis is a vascular organ, conditions that affect your arteries and veins can impact your erection. If there is something going on with your vascular system that is affecting your sexual performance, there could also be issues with your heart or lower extremity blood vessels.

It’s important to share your medical and family history with the urologist and provide information on what any other doctors are treating you for. Many men who have had prostate issues, or any type of radiation treatment or surgery, believe that ED is a normal result of that condition, and are unaware that there are effective treatments available.

You should never feel rushed into a quick fix, without a thorough assessment of your health and treatment recommendations tailored specifically to your needs. It's important to feel comfortable with your treatment choice after you've considered all the options available to you.

Addressing male sexual dysfunction begins with health management and positive lifestyle changes. If that doesn’t fix the issue, prescription medications for ED are the next step. In combination with diet, exercise, smoking cessation and taking your other prescribed medications, they can help restore sexual function. If that doesn’t work, there are intracorporeal injections. Patients try it in the doctor’s office, and are trained to do this at home. Using a very fine needle, like the one people who have diabetes use for insulin, medication is injected directly into the penis 20 minutes before sex. This works best for men who live at home and don’t travel too much because the medication requires refrigeration. It’s effective and efficient, particularly for men in stable relationships. If a year passes and that treatment is no longer satisfactory, the next step is a penile prosthesis. The outpatient surgery takes about an hour and the implant lasts for 10 to 15 years. After recovery, men maintain the same sensation and sexual response they had before, and nothing shows because the entire implant is internal.

Working with you to protect and restore your sexual and overall health

Montefiore maintains a robust men’s health program that takes an interdisciplinary approach to the treatment of ED. With our emphasis on diagnosis and treatment of any underlying causes of sexual dysfunction, our urologists routinely refer patients for primary care, as well as to specialists in cardiology and endocrinology when appropriate. Family-centered care is a priority, and men are encouraged to involve their spouses or partners in care decisions every step of the way. Montefiore has one of the busiest implant centers in the region, enhanced by our collaborative approach to men’s health.

Educating the community is a priority at Montefiore, reminding men to see a doctor if you have any signs of sexual dysfunction or prostate issues. The specialists at Montefiore are not judgmental. They’re there to help, because there is no need for a man to be impotent or miss getting checked for men’s health problems. Tell the doctors everything that you feel, even if you think is not important, because it may improve or even save your life.
