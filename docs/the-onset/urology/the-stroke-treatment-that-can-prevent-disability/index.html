The stroke treatment that can prevent disability

By David J. Altschul, MD, Dir. of Neurovascular Surgery, Co-Dir. of Montefiore Comprehensive Stroke Center; Allan Brook, MD, Neuroradiology; Neil Haranhalli, MD, Endovascular/Cerebrovascular Neurosurgeon; & Daniel Labovitz, MD, Vascular Neurology · 4 min read · El tratamiento de accidente cerebrovascular que previene la discapacidad

In short

Innovations in stroke diagnostics and treatment techniques are saving brains — and lives — and making it possible to reverse disability from stroke. Since most strokes are “ischemic,” or caused by a blood clot in the brain, the earlier we can use a procedure called thrombectomy to remove a clot and restore blood flow to the brain, the better our chances are of restoring brain function and preventing long-term disability.

Saving brains and reversing disability from stroke

Montefiore Einstein is a recognized leader in saving brains and preventing disability and is the first health system from Northern Manhattan to Albany — and among the elite 3% of hospitals in the nation — to earn the prestigious advanced Comprehensive Stroke Center designation from the Joint Commission.

Treating more strokes with the treatment that prevents long-term disability

One in six people will suffer a stroke at some point in their lifetime, and quick action saves lives.

Continued advancements in thrombectomy are allowing us to treat more strokes than ever before to prevent and even reverse disability. Thrombectomy also expands the treatment window to up to 24 hours after a stroke occurs, quickly and effectively removing clots and restoring blood flow to the brain as soon as possible. While clot-busting medications like TNK, or tenecteplase, remain an important treatment for acute stroke, particularly for milder cases, they’re not always enough to clear large blockages. Thrombectomy may be used in addition to TNK or instead of it, as needed.

Our continuous research and leadership in clinical trials has propelled advancements in the practice, technology, and application of thrombectomy, giving physicians more time to save more brains.

A successful thrombectomy enables us to immediately see the signs of reversing a stroke in a patient. Something as small as a patient starting to move their leg again for the first time will have the surgery team cheering, and when the clot comes out, it is truly exhilarating. Having a patient come back to see us after going through rehab and saying, “Thank you so much, I’m back at work,” is what makes us wake up at 3 o’clock in the morning excited to care for our patients.

Computer tomography (CT) imaging assisted by the latest AI allows us to pinpoint the location of a blockage faster, assess tissue damage, and immediately determine the viability of a thrombectomy. This makes it possible for a growing number of patients to benefit from thrombectomy. The ability to reduce disability — allowing patients to walk and speak to their families again, regaining their quality of life — inspires our efforts to get stroke patients where they need to be as quickly as possible to receive the treatment they need. Thrombectomy is available only at hospitals that, like Montefiore Einstein, are designated advanced Comprehensive Stroke Centers.

Recognize the signs of stroke and BE FAST: “Time is brain”

One in six people will suffer a stroke at some point in their lifetime, and quick action saves lives. BE FAST is a great way to remember the most common symptoms of stroke and what to do if someone, including yourself, is experiencing any of them.

E – Eyes: Have they lost vision in one or both eyes?

F – Face: Does one side of their face droop when they are asked to smile?

A – Arms: Does one arm drift downward when they are asked to raise both arms?

S – Speech: Is their speech slurred when they are asked to repeat a simple phrase?

T – Time: Call 911 immediately if you observe any of these signs

Whenever a stroke is suspected, call 911 immediately. Don’t worry about whether or not your diagnosis is correct. The most important thing is to get to a designated comprehensive stroke center as quickly as possible.

Montefiore Einstein: The first designated advanced Comprehensive Stroke Center from Northern Manhattan to Albany

Individual hospitals have different levels of care available for those experiencing a stroke. The Joint Commission has designated Montefiore Einstein as an advanced Comprehensive Stroke Center (CSC). It’s one of the only CSCs from Northern Manhattan to Albany and is among the elite 3% of hospitals in the nation to earn this designation. To be certified as a Comprehensive Stroke Center, the center must provide state-of-the-art care for all neurovascular conditions, including stroke, aneurysms, and arteriovenous malformations (AVMs), while following the most current treatment guidelines, achieving outstanding outcomes, and consistently demonstrating leadership in research that advances effective treatments that give stroke patients the best chance for a full recovery. Our physicians’ mastery of these state-of-the-art tools and technologies — along with leadership in pioneering thrombectomy clinical trials — is enabling us to remove clots in blood vessels previously considered too small to safely access. This foundational work continues to expand the range of patients who can benefit from this disability-reversing treatment.

Once treated, as soon as patients start improving, they receive state-of-the-art physical therapy and rehabilitation care at Montefiore Einstein’s Burke Rehabilitation Hospital, in Westchester. Studies have shown that this form of multidisciplinary care greatly improves patient outcomes.

We’re ranked in the top 1% of all U.S. hospitals for neurology and neurosurgery, and Montefiore Einstein’s Burke Rehabilitation Hospital is also ranked in the top 1% of all U.S. hospitals for rehabilitation, according to U.S. News & World Report. Additionally, we are recipients of the highest standard for rehabilitation medicine, a three-year designation granted by the Commission on Accreditation of Rehabilitation Facilities (CARF).
