Hidden causes of erectile dysfunction: Dispelling ED myths

By Pedro Maria, DO, Attending Physician, Urology · 3 min read · Las causas ocultas de la disfunción eréctil: Acabar con los mitos de la disfunción eréctil

In short

It’s no surprise that many people are uncomfortable talking to a doctor about erectile dysfunction. The shame and embarrassment that people with this condition often feel can affect relationships, cause anxiety or depression, and make many feel isolated. By treating the hidden cause of this common condition, we can not only reverse it but also improve a man’s general health and well-being.

Get your sex life back: Treating erectile dysfunction’s hidden causes

How? Erectile dysfunction is most often caused by an underlying health condition that’s causing other problems as well. And while many people believe the myth that erectile dysfunction is a normal part of aging, men of any age can suffer from it. The truth is it’s almost always reversible and treatable if caught early, since treating the cause can prevent damage to critical veins — damage that, if ignored, can lead to permanent dysfunction. Understanding and addressing erectile dysfunction can also improve a man’s mental health and relationships.

Why erectile dysfunction is not a “normal” part of aging

For many years, it was thought that erectile dysfunction was largely a psychological issue. We now know better. More than 30 million Americans experience the condition, and it’s not related to age or ethnicity. And while erectile dysfunction does become more common as a man gets older, it’s because the health conditions that cause it typically become more prevalent over time.

While these are serious conditions, they respond well to treatment in most cases, and early diagnosis leads to better outcomes. This is particularly important for younger men, in whom erectile dysfunction can be an early warning sign of heart or vascular disease. Up to 40% of men who visit a urologist don’t mention that they have issues with erectile dysfunction unless the doctor asks about it, so challenge yourself to put any shame or embarrassment aside if you notice a problem.

Beyond these conditions, erectile dysfunction can also be caused by hormone imbalances, neurological issues, mental health issues such as depression and anxiety, trauma, medications (including some designed to treat depression, high blood pressure, Parkinson’s disease, and allergies), and alcohol and recreational drugs.

There’s a lot of misunderstanding about over-the-counter remedies that promise to “cure” erectile dysfunction. Although some may have an immediate effect, miracle cures, “sexual enhancement” products, and counterfeit medications can be dangerous. Pills may be laced with harmful drugs or other ingredients but still look nearly identical to their authentic counterparts. The conditions that cause erectile dysfunction are also often progressive and become more difficult to treat over time, so a quick-fix pill is not a viable treatment.

A primary care doctor or urologist can diagnose hidden health issues like a vascular condition, which could indicate an underlying heart disease. There are treatments that can repair damaged blood vessels as well as heart conditions. If the cause is determined to be a hormone imbalance, diabetes, or a mental health condition, there are many treatment options. There are also medications, injections, and implants that can restore normal sexual function. However, the best way to keep erectile dysfunction at bay is through prevention. That means choosing healthy habits — including regular exercise, a healthy diet, and not smoking — as well as taking medications for other health conditions, paying attention to signs of prostate issues, and getting regular check-ups.
