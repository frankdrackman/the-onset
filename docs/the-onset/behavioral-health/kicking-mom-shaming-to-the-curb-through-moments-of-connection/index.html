Kicking mom-shaming to the curb through moments of connection

By Amanda Zayde, Psy.D, Behavioral Health · 4 min read · Momentos de conexión para superar las críticas a las madres

In short

On Mother’s Day, we celebrate the love, empathy, and strength of moms. And yet mom-shaming remains commonplace year-round. Others all too often criticize mothers for their parenting styles, and it’s easy to do it to ourselves as well. While it’s normal for moms to reflect on their assumptions and beliefs about raising a child — and look for ways to grow as a parent — now is a good time to take a breath and recalibrate.

One big step for momkind: Less mom-shaming, more kindness

This Mother’s Day is a great opportunity to celebrate your resilience, and reflect on your remarkable capacity to continue to do your best, while also exploring ways you can show yourself the same compassion you show others. Here are a few insights and tips.

Being perfect is an impossible goal for any mom

Moms worried about the impact of stress on their children are often surprised to learn that their own ability to handle stress is one of the most significant predictors for how their child will function during, and after, difficult times. But that doesn’t mean you have to try to be a perfect parent every minute of every day. Those days when you feel overwhelmed or have a short-fuse moment offer an opportunity to model things like apologizing and taking accountability, as well as asking for support. These experiences can fuel important conversations with your children and show them how to navigate and overcome stressful situations on their own.

“Mom-shaming” is among the top challenges described by mothers. Criticism from oneself and others can undermine confidence, and even helpful suggestions can be taken the wrong way. Mother’s Day is a good day to recognize that we are all simply making the best decisions possible in challenging situations. What’s right for your family may not work for another family. This is one area where extended family and community members can really support mothers — by suspending judgment and engaging with them in more supportive ways. Compassion is key. A small gesture toward a mother, such as complimenting her on her parenting skills or praising her children, can go a long way. So, moms, please acknowledge and celebrate your own resilience and accomplishments each day as a way to be kinder to yourselves, and perhaps even look for ways to celebrate the moms you know.

The idea of self-care may seem like an additional chore when you already feel like there aren’t enough hours in the day. Whether you need a little time, space, or just someone to listen, asking for that support is a key part of your self-care plan.

Small things you do for yourself in 5- or 10-minute bursts are self-care: taking a short walk, listening to your favorite song, connecting with that friend or family member who you know is in your corner, or just closing your eyes to breathe for a few minutes. Mindfully pausing to observe your own emotions is another powerful self-care tool. Sometimes, simply realizing that you are feeling overwhelmed, guilty, afraid, or angry is enough to take the edge off and bring you back to a place that is less fraught.

Routine is one way to feel more in charge. Routine has the effect of making us feel safe, and it’s something the whole family can work on together, including carving out some mom time. Some moms find it helpful to write in a journal every night, recording the things they’re grateful for; some connect with friends quickly via text before bedtime; others savor a piece of dark chocolate every day; some have a morning stretching routine that reduces stress and starts their day off right. The most important self-care activities are the ones that you can find a way to do every day. And if you miss a day, don’t worry! Just try again tomorrow.

Kindness is a gift we can all give moms every day

As we move beyond Mother’s Day, it’s important to remember that millions of moms are navigating challenging times. A compassionate statement or friendly, nonjudgmental ear can be a gift to any busy mother. Kindness is free, and moms need all that we can all offer.
