Celebrating moms for their extraordinary resilience

By Dr. Amanda Zayde, for Montefiore-Einstein, Behavioral Health · 4 min read

In short

This month is a special celebration of all moms, as you have had to show such resilience and courage over the past year. You’ve shouldered tremendous added responsibility since the start of the COVID-19 pandemic, making difficult decisions about sending children back to school, managing remote learning, and helping your children cope with the disruption of their lives. Many of you had to do all this while working from home or had to sacrifice a job in order to be there for your children. And let’s not overlook the moms who gave birth during the past year, many going it alone through the delivery and early days with their newborns. The hardships of the past year have led many moms to question their assumptions and beliefs about parenting, while mom-shaming remains commonplace. It’s time to take a breath and recalibrate.

This Mother’s Day is a great opportunity to celebrate your resilience, reflect on your remarkable ability to get through it all, and explore ways to gain the help you need to navigate what’s ahead. Here are a few insights and tips for tackling the year to come.

Moms worried about the pandemic’s long-term impacts on their children are often surprised to learn that their own ability to handle stress is one of the most significant predictors for how their child will function during and after difficult times like these. But that doesn’t mean you have to be the perfect parent every minute of every day. Those days when you feel overwhelmed or have a short-fuse moment offer an opportunity to model things like apologizing and taking accountability, as well as asking for support. These experiences can fuel important conversations with your children and show them how to navigate and overcome stressful situations of their own.

“Mom-shaming” is among the top challenges described by mothers and may be even more commonplace during the pandemic. Criticism from oneself and others can undermine confidence, and even helpful suggestions can be taken the wrong way. Mother’s Day is a good day to recognize that we are all simply making the best decisions possible in challenging situations. What’s right for your family may not work for another family. This is one area where extended family and community members can really support mothers - by suspending judgment and engaging with them in more supportive ways. Kindness is key. A small gesture toward a mother, such as complimenting her on parenting skills or praising her children, can go a long way. So moms, please acknowledge and celebrate your own resilience and accomplishments each day as a way to be kinder to yourselves, and perhaps even look for ways to celebrate the moms you know.

The idea of self-care may seem like an additional chore when you’re already feeling there are not enough hours in the day. Whether you need a little time, space or just someone to listen, asking for that support is a key part of your self-care plan.

Small things you do for yourself in five or 10-minute bursts are self-care: taking a short walk, listening to your favorite song, connecting with that friend or family member who you know is in your corner, or just closing your eyes to breathe for a few minutes. Mindfully pausing to observe your own emotions is another powerful self-care tool. Sometimes, simply realizing that you are feeling overwhelmed, guilty, afraid or angry is enough to take the edge off and bring you back to a place that is less fraught.

At a time when so much is beyond our control, routine is one way to feel more in charge. Routine has the effect of making us feel safe, and it’s something the whole family can work on together, including carving out some mom time. Some moms find it helpful to write in a journal every night, recording the things they’re grateful for; others savor a piece of dark chocolate every day; some have a morning stretching routine that reduces stress and starts their day off right. The most important self-care activities are the ones that you can find a way to do every day. And if you miss a day, don’t worry! Just start again tomorrow.

Montefiore-Einstein CARE Program offers connection and support

Many moms are finding that support groups, facilitated by experienced professionals, are exactly what they need right now. Moms feel validated by talking through their concerns with others in a setting where they can freely express themselves, share their stories, and benefit from exchanging resources and tips with other group members.

Montefiore-Einstein’s CARE (Connecting And Reflecting Experience) program is a group-based initiative designed to support parents and other primary caregivers. We emphasize creating a safe, judgment-free place for parents to connect, encourage one another through parenting challenges, and assist each other in better understanding the needs of their children. We also encourage reflection on the parenting they have received and how it has shaped them as a caregiver today. Mothers report feeling less alone in their parenting experience as a result of their participation in a CARE group. If you’re struggling with the challenges of parenting, use the link above to reach out to CARE. New groups are forming, and you may find just what you need.

As we move beyond Mother’s Day, it’s important to remember that millions of moms are still navigating challenging times. That kind statement or a friendly non-judgmental ear can be a gift to any busy mom. Kindness is free, and today more than ever, mothers and children need all that we can offer to them.
